## Purpose

The theorems of batch 0021 are about a Lean definition of the forward pass of a decoder, and the correspondence between that definition and `model.py` (the definition that the experiments of batches 0015 and 0019 ran, which was checked against Hugging Face's Llama) was "by reading". It rests on conventions that are easy to get wrong without any theorem noticing: which rows of `wq` are the plane `p` of head `a`, which group a head reads, how the output matrix is cut into heads, the order of the two components of a rotary plane, where the norm weight multiplies, what is permuted when the heads are permuted. A wrong convention would make every theorem true of another model. With head dimension 2 there is one rotary plane and the rows of its two components are adjacent, so the rotate-half pairing `(i, i + hd/2)` and the interleaved pairing `(2i, 2i + 1)` coincide and there is no plane index to get wrong: the configurations of head dimension 2 test the other conventions (groups, the cut of the output matrix, the placement of the norm weight, the permutations) but not the pairing or the order of planes. The configuration `d2p2` (head dimension 4, two planes, three positions) is for those.

This batch runs the Lean definition. A random model in the layout of `model.py` (the matrices `out × in` of Hugging Face's Llama, the heads and the planes in the flat rows) is given to Lean as data; `toLayer` of `Runtime.lean`, which is the correspondence in code, turns it into the structured layers of `Model.lean`; Lean's interpreter evaluates `layerFn`, `qv`, `kv`, `vv` and `logits`, the definitions that the theorems are about, and prints every array exactly. The same model goes through `model.py`'s `forward` and through the transformations of `gauge.py`, in the exact arithmetic of `ops.FieldOps`; and Lean's own definitions of the gauges (`streamGauge`, `ovGauge`, `qkGauge`, `headPerm`, `unitPerm`, `mlpScale`, `attnNorm`, `mlpNorm`) are applied to the structured layers and the result printed. The two sets of arrays are compared for equality.

The field is `F_p` with `p = 1000003` (`Runtime.lean` proves by `norm_num` that it is prime, so that `ZMod p` is a field and the theorems apply to it). Not the rationals: the nonlinearities of `FieldOps` (a square; the reciprocal of a square plus three) double the degree of the rational function at every application, so that exact rationals have thousands of digits after two layers (this was tried: the exact forward pass of `model.py` on `Fraction`s did not finish in ten minutes for a model of 105 weights per layer). Over `F_p` the numbers stay below `p`. Agreement of two arrays at a random draw is evidence that the two programs are equal; no error probability is claimed (the degree of the logits as polynomials in the weights is far above `p = 1000003`, so that Schwartz–Zippel, which batch 0015 uses with `p = 2^61 - 1`, gives nothing at this `p`).

The registered model (`d3`): hidden dimension 3; 4 query heads in 2 key/value groups (two heads per group); head dimension 2 (one rotary plane, the rotation of position `n` being the `n`-th power of the point `((1 - t²)/(1 + t²), 2t/(1 + t²))` of the circle, `t = 2`); gated block with 3 units; vocabulary 3; 2 layers; no tied head; 2 tokens `[2, 0]`; the nonlinearities of `FieldOps` (exp s = s² + s + 1, silu s = s² + 2s, the scale of the norm `1/(m² + 3)`, the scale of the scores 7). The configuration `d2` has hidden dimension 2 and 2 units (the rest as `d3`), and `d2p2` has head dimension 4 (planes at `t = 2` and `t = 3`) and 3 tokens `[2, 0, 1]`. The seed is the first (from `--start`) for which the exact arithmetic of Python says that the logits of every transformed model equal the original's and that the table of batch 0021 holds on the arrays printed (so that "differs" is not an accident of a degenerate draw); the draws are selected in this way, not unselected random models.

## What is run, and what is compared

- The original model, and the same model with its norm weights folded into the matrices that read them (`fold_norms`; the base of the gauge H4, which needs constant norm weights): weights, caches, streams, logits.
- The original model cut to one layer, whose logits are computed by the definition `logits` itself (no tables of intermediate results), and by the layer-by-layer runner of `Runtime.lean`.
- Seven transformed models, each made by Lean's definitions from the structured layers: H1 (`streamGauge` with a signed permutation of the three hidden coordinates, norm weights permuted), H4 (`streamGauge` with an orthogonal matrix, a product of two reflections, on the folded model), A1 (`ovGauge`), A3 (`qkGauge`), A6 (`headPerm`: groups and heads), M1 (`unitPerm` of `mlpScale`), N1 (`mlpNorm` of `attnNorm`; the final norm is left alone), with parameters drawn at random, different in the two layers.
- For each model Lean prints the weights in the flat layout of `model.py`, the queries, keys, values of each layer (from the stream that enters it), the stream after each layer, and the logits. `check22.py` compares them to `expected.txt` (written by `gen22.py` from `model.py` and `gauge.py`): 278 arrays.

## Predictions, with severity

- **R1, moderate severity.** For the original model and the folded model, all 64 arrays that Lean prints (42 weight arrays after the round trip flat → structured → flat, which checks `toLayer` against its own inverse; 22 arrays of queries, keys, values, stream and logits) are equal to those of `model.py`.
- **R2, moderate severity.** For the model cut to one layer, the logits by the definition `logits` itself, the logits of the runner, and those of `model.py` are equal (3 arrays).
- **R3, high severity (it is the correspondence of the definitions of the gauges with `gauge.py`, and conventions of permutations and transposes are where I make mistakes).** For each of the seven gauges, the weights of the model that Lean's definition produces, printed in the flat layout, are equal to those that `gauge.py` produces from the same parameters: 7 × 21 = 147 arrays.
- **R4, moderate severity.** For each gauge, the queries, keys, values, streams and logits that Lean computes for its transformed model are equal to those of `model.py` run on `gauge.py`'s transformed parameters: 7 × 9 = 63 arrays.
- **R5, low severity (it is a consequence of the theorems, and of R3 and R4; Lean against Lean).** For each gauge the logits of Lean's transformed model are equal to those of its original model (the folded model for H4): 7 comparisons.
- **R6, low severity.** The permutations that Lean builds from products of transpositions (the permutation of the hidden coordinates, and of the heads in each layer) are those that `gen22.py` intended: 2 arrays.
- **R7, moderate severity (Lean against Lean).** For each gauge, Lean's arrays for the stream after the first layer, and for the queries, keys and values of the first layer, differ from the original's exactly where the table of batch 0021 says "moved" and are equal where it says "fixed": 28 comparisons (H1, H4: stream differs, cache equal; A1: values differ, the rest equal; A3: queries and keys differ, the rest equal; A6: queries, keys and values differ, stream equal; M1, N1: all equal).

R5 and R7 have counterparts on the Python side that hold for the chosen seed (the generator checks them before writing the file). The run prints each array as soon as it is computed, so that what was computed is graded and the rest is `not run`. A mismatch is a result: it says that a Lean definition is not the Python one, in the array named.

## What this does not do

- It does not test the theorems; it tests that the objects they are about are the objects that were experimented on, on a few random instances of small architectures: two groups of two heads, two layers, three tokens of vocabulary, two or three positions, an untied head, RMS normalization; one rotary plane in `d3` and `d2` (so the pairing of the rotary components and the order of planes are not tested there; `d2p2` has two planes). Not covered: a tied head, LayerNorm, partial rotary embeddings, `T` larger than the number of positions printed. In `d2` and `d2p2` the hidden dimension is 2, where the orthogonal matrix of H4 (a product of two reflections) is a special family.
- It does not run a real checkpoint or floating point: `F_p` has no order, no `exp` and no square root; the nonlinearities are the polynomial surrogates of `ops.py`, as in batch 0015.
- It does not compare Lean with Hugging Face's Llama (batch 0015's `conform.py` ties `model.py` to Hugging Face's on float64; this batch ties Lean to `model.py`; the chain has those two links).
- It does not test the Lean gauge definitions beyond the seven gauges and one draw of parameters each; the final-norm scaling of `gauge.py`'s `norm_gauge` is not run (the scale is the identity there).

## Grading

`check22.py` reads `lean_out.txt` and `expected.txt` and writes `results22.md` and `results22.json`: for each of R1 to R7, the number of arrays compared, equal, mismatching and not run. The prediction R_n holds if all arrays of its category are equal (or as the table) and none is `not run`.

**What this establishes.** The Lean definitions of batch 0021 (the forward pass `layerFn`, `qv`, `kv`, `vv`, `logits`, and the gauges `streamGauge`, `ovGauge`, `qkGauge`, `headPerm`, `unitPerm`, `mlpScale`, `attnNorm`, `mlpNorm`) are, at four instances over `F_p` with p = 1000003 and head dimension 2, the forward pass of `model.py` and the transformations of `gauge.py` of batch 0015, in every array printed; the convention by which a structured layer (per head, per plane) is read from the flat matrices of Hugging Face's layout is the code `toLayer`, for head dimension 2. With the theorems of batch 0021 (true of the Lean definition for every field and every nonlinearity), the chain from the theorem to the runs is: theorem, Lean definition, this batch (Lean definition equal to `model.py` and `gauge.py` on four instances), batch 0015 (`model.py` equal to Hugging Face's Llama in float64), batch 0014 (the symmetric variants in five runtimes). Each link is a test or a proof of the kind named, not the same kind.

**What it does not establish.** Equality at four instances of small architectures is evidence that the programs are equal, not a proof and not a bound: the degree of the logits as polynomials in the weights is far above p = 1000003, so that Schwartz–Zippel gives nothing at this p. It does not test the pairing of the two components of a rotary plane (rotate-half against interleaved) nor the order of several planes: with head dimension 2 these coincide; the configuration `d2p2` is for that, and has not been run. It covers: untied head, RMS normalization, one rotary plane, 2 groups of 2 heads, T = 2, two layers. A tied head, LayerNorm, several rotary planes (until `d2p2` is run), partial rotary embeddings, longer sequences are not run. The field has no order, no `exp` and no square root: the nonlinearities are the polynomial surrogates; the Lean theorems do not care, and the equality of Lean with `model.py` over `F_p` does not tell that the real nonlinearities are implemented as Hugging Face does (batch 0015's `conform.py` does, in float64, for `model.py`).
