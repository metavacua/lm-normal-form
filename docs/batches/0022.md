## Purpose

The theorems of batch 0021 are about a Lean definition of the forward pass of a decoder, and the correspondence between that definition and `model.py` (the definition that the experiments of batches 0015 and 0019 ran, which was checked against Hugging Face's Llama) was "by reading". It rests on conventions that are easy to get wrong without any theorem noticing: which rows of `wq` are the plane `p` of head `a`, which group a head reads, how the output matrix is cut into heads, the order of the two components of a rotary plane, where the norm weight multiplies, what is permuted when the heads are permuted. A wrong convention would make every theorem true of another model.

This batch runs the Lean definition. A random model in the layout of `model.py` (the matrices `out × in` of Hugging Face's Llama, the heads and the planes in the flat rows) is given to Lean as data; `toLayer` of `Runtime.lean`, which is the correspondence in code, turns it into the structured layers of `Model.lean`; Lean's interpreter evaluates `layerFn`, `qv`, `kv`, `vv` and `logits`, the definitions that the theorems are about, and prints every array exactly. The same model goes through `model.py`'s `forward` and through the transformations of `gauge.py`, in the exact arithmetic of `ops.FieldOps`; and Lean's own definitions of the gauges (`streamGauge`, `ovGauge`, `qkGauge`, `headPerm`, `unitPerm`, `mlpScale`, `attnNorm`, `mlpNorm`) are applied to the structured layers and the result printed. The two sets of arrays are compared for equality.

The field is `F_p` with `p = 1000003` (`Runtime.lean` proves by `norm_num` that it is prime, so that `ZMod p` is a field and the theorems apply to it). Not the rationals: the nonlinearities of `FieldOps` (a square; the reciprocal of a square plus three) double the degree of the rational function at every application, so that exact rationals have thousands of digits after two layers (this was tried: the exact forward pass of `model.py` on `Fraction`s did not finish in ten minutes for a model of 105 weights per layer). Over `F_p` the numbers stay below `p`, and agreement of two arrays at a random draw is evidence of equality of the functions with error probability about `p^-n` for `n` entries (Schwartz–Zippel, as in batch 0015).

The model: hidden dimension 3; 4 query heads in 2 key/value groups (two heads per group); head dimension 2 (one rotary plane, the rotation of position `n` being the `n`-th power of the point `((1 - t²)/(1 + t²), 2t/(1 + t²))` of the circle, `t = 2`); gated block with 3 units; vocabulary 3; 2 layers; no tied head; 2 tokens `[2, 0]`; the nonlinearities of `FieldOps` (exp s = s² + s + 1, silu s = s² + 2s, the scale of the norm `1/(m² + 3)`, the scale of the scores 7). The seed is the first (from 0) for which the exact arithmetic of Python says that the logits of every transformed model equal the original's and that the table of batch 0021 holds on the arrays printed (so that "differs" is not an accident of a degenerate draw).

## What is run, and what is compared

- The original model, and the same model with its norm weights folded into the matrices that read them (`fold_norms`; the base of the gauge H4, which needs constant norm weights): weights, caches, streams, logits.
- The original model cut to one layer, whose logits are computed by the definition `logits` itself (no tables of intermediate results), and by the layer-by-layer runner of `Runtime.lean`.
- Seven transformed models, each made by Lean's definitions from the structured layers: H1 (`streamGauge` with a signed permutation of the three hidden coordinates, norm weights permuted), H4 (`streamGauge` with an orthogonal matrix, a product of two reflections, on the folded model), A1 (`ovGauge`), A3 (`qkGauge`), A6 (`headPerm`: groups and heads), M1 (`unitPerm` of `mlpScale`), N1 (`mlpNorm` of `attnNorm`; the final norm is left alone), with parameters drawn at random, different in the two layers.
- For each model Lean prints the weights in the flat layout of `model.py`, the queries, keys, values of each layer (from the stream that enters it), the stream after each layer, and the logits. `check22.py` compares them to `expected.txt` (written by `gen22.py` from `model.py` and `gauge.py`): 278 arrays.

## Predictions, with severity

- **R1, moderate severity.** For the original model and the folded model, all 64 arrays that Lean prints (weights after the round trip flat → structured → flat; queries, keys, values and stream of both layers; logits) are equal to those of `model.py`.
- **R2, moderate severity.** For the model cut to one layer, the logits by the definition `logits` itself, the logits of the runner, and those of `model.py` are equal (3 arrays).
- **R3, high severity (it is the correspondence of the definitions of the gauges with `gauge.py`, and conventions of permutations and transposes are where I make mistakes).** For each of the seven gauges, the weights of the model that Lean's definition produces, printed in the flat layout, are equal to those that `gauge.py` produces from the same parameters: 7 × 21 = 147 arrays.
- **R4, moderate severity.** For each gauge, the queries, keys, values, streams and logits that Lean computes for its transformed model are equal to those of `model.py` run on `gauge.py`'s transformed parameters: 7 × 9 = 63 arrays.
- **R5, low severity (it is a consequence of the theorems, and of R3 and R4).** For each gauge the logits of Lean's transformed model are equal to those of its original model (the folded model for H4): 7 comparisons.
- **R6, low severity.** The permutations that Lean builds from products of transpositions (the permutation of the hidden coordinates, and of the heads in each layer) are those that `gen22.py` intended: 2 arrays.
- **R7, moderate severity.** For each gauge, Lean's arrays for the stream after the first layer, and for the queries, keys and values of the first layer, differ from the original's exactly where the table of batch 0021 says "moved" and are equal where it says "fixed": 28 comparisons (H1, H4: stream differs, cache equal; A1: values differ, the rest equal; A3: queries and keys differ, the rest equal; A6: queries, keys and values differ, stream equal; M1, N1: all equal).

R5 and R7 have counterparts on the Python side that hold for the chosen seed (the generator checks them before writing the file); the Lean side is not known.

I expect the first build or run to fail somewhere (the runner is new Lean code; the interpreter may be slower than I estimate: the run is limited to 150 minutes and prints each array as soon as it is computed, so that what was computed is graded and the rest is `not run`). A mismatch is a result: it says that a Lean definition is not the Python one, in the array named.

## What this does not do

- It does not test the theorems; it tests that the objects they are about are the objects that were experimented on, on one random instance of one small architecture: one rotary plane, two groups of two heads, two layers, three tokens of vocabulary, two positions, an untied head, RMS normalization. Not covered: a tied head, LayerNorm, several rotary planes, partial rotary embeddings, `T` larger than the number of positions printed.
- It does not run a real checkpoint or floating point: `F_p` has no order, no `exp` and no square root; the nonlinearities are the polynomial surrogates of `ops.py`, as in batch 0015.
- It does not compare Lean with Hugging Face's Llama (batch 0015's `conform.py` ties `model.py` to Hugging Face's on float64; this batch ties Lean to `model.py`; the chain has those two links).
- It does not test the Lean gauge definitions beyond the seven gauges and one draw of parameters each; the final-norm scaling of `gauge.py`'s `norm_gauge` is not run (the scale is the identity there).

## Faults and amendments

- **Run 37553841380 (commit 44afa2a).** The Lean build was accepted at the first attempt, including the new module `Runtime.lean` (and its proof by `norm_num` that 1000003 is prime), and the generator ran; the Lean evaluation had not finished after 25 minutes, when I stopped waiting (the next push cancels it). The estimate of the cost that I made when I registered was wrong: the definitions of `Model.lean` are functions, not tables, so that a subterm that the formulas use several times (the normalized stream, the attention output) is recomputed at every use, and the interpreter runs generic code with dictionaries for the field operations; counting the operations of one entry of the layer function, the model of this registration (hidden dimension 3, three units) costs of the order of 3e8 field operations per layer, which at a microsecond each is minutes per layer and an hour for the definitional logits of one layer. I did not measure it. The order of the printing made it worse: the most expensive array was printed before the gauged models. Amendment 1: the hidden dimension is 2 and the number of units 2 (the other dimensions are unchanged: 4 query heads in 2 groups, one rotary plane, vocabulary 3, 2 layers, 2 positions), which cuts the cost of a layer by a factor of about six; the printing is by groups of models, each group in its own process, four processes at once, the cheap arrays first and the definitional logits last, with a line of timing after each model (`check22.py` ignores the lines that begin with `# `). The registered predictions (R1 to R7) and the counts of arrays (278) are unchanged; the description of the model in the purpose section (hidden dimension 3, three units) is the registered one and is not edited.
