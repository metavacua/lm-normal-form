## Review of batch 0013 (the reason for this batch)

Asked for: a critical and constructive review of batch 0013, and the comparison of the models themselves, before and after the row and column operations, in several runtimes.

**What batch 0013 established, as far as its evidence goes.** For five Llama-architecture models: one matrix at a time (independent row and column permutations) the three float models are in the special case
and the two ternary models are not; at the level of the network's own index spaces all five are; a sorted canonical form is bit-exact against random elements of the group of signed permutations and powers of two and against
random signs of zeros, and sees four non-symmetries; the first form of it was refuted on TriLM 390M for a reason that was found (173 all-zero rotary planes, and the sign bit of zero) and amended. The predictions were registered
before the runs and graded by a program written before the results were read; the refutation and the amendments are on the record.

**What it did not establish, in the order of how much it matters.**

1. *That the transformed models work, as language models, anywhere but in one place.* The function evidence (P11, P16) is one model (SmolLM2-135M-Instruct), one runtime (PyTorch/Transformers, float32, CPU, one processor type), 63
   sequences of 982 token positions in all (median 16 tokens, at most 21), one random group element per part (seed 13). There is no generation, no perplexity on a standard text, no chat template, no second model, no second runtime.
   "The canonical form is a model" rests on one draw. The strongest sentence the evidence allows is: *in one runtime, on 982 positions, the transformed weights of one model gave logits within 2.3e-4 of the original's*.
2. *Files.* The transformed weights were float32 arrays loaded into a model in memory. The artifacts that people use are bfloat16 and float16 files. Sign flips, powers of two and permutations leave a bfloat16 value on its grid, so a transformed
   checkpoint should be a drop-in replacement in the original dtype, but "should" is not a run: nothing was written to a `safetensors` file and loaded by anything.
3. *Independence.* The transformations and the canonical form were written by one hand, and the check that they are symmetries ran on one implementation of the model. The class of error that a second, unrelated implementation catches (a
   convention that PyTorch and my code share, a layout that one runtime reads differently, tied embeddings handled differently by a converter) was not looked for. P11's "byte-identical logits" is a property of PyTorch's CPU kernels at one
   thread count, not of the algebra.
4. *Performance.* Nothing about the speed, memory or size of the transformed models was measured; the only timings are of the cells. Whether a permutation changes throughput through cache behaviour, and whether it changes the quality of a
   block-quantized model, was open. The second matters: block quantization is not invariant to every row and column operation, and it is the form in which small models are mostly run.
5. *The invariance was tested at n = 1.* One random element per part; no spread of errors; no variation of thread count, batch size or padding.
6. *What the 74 lines say.* Many of them are controls or near-certain (the float models having no ties; a self-test passing; roots of an unchanged file being equal). The information is in about ten of them: the ternary ties (P2), the exactness of
   the canonical form and its refutation on one model (P8, P12), the negative controls (P9), the function numbers (P11, P16), the census. A count of lines graded "as predicted" says less than it seems to.
7. *The canonical form is of the discrete part of the symmetry group only.* The norm-weight gauge, the continuous value/output gauge GL(d_head), the complex scalars of the query/key planes, the stream scale and the rotations are not
   normalized, so "identity up to symmetry" does not identify two models that are related by them. The batch said so; the name and the title can be over-read.
8. *No refusal.* The code assumes a Llama-style model and does not check it (query/key norms, biases, sliding windows, soft-capping would have been processed as if they were not there). The amended form raises on some degenerate weights
   and nothing raises on an unsupported architecture. The five models happened to be supported.
9. *P12 for TriLM 390M was not blind*, and the raising paths were exercised only on toy models.
10. *No record per model.* The results are in a long document; there is no one file that says, for a model, what was checked and what passed.
11. *The state between the weights and the logits was not looked at.* A runtime stores, for every layer and token, keys and values: the KV cache. The batch compared weights and logits; what the symmetries do to the cache, and which of them a quantized cache survives, is
    the part of the computation that runtimes keep, move, offload and quantize, and it was open.

## Purpose

Batch 0013 showed, on paper and in one runtime, that the row and column operations that are symmetries of a Llama-style Transformer (permutations of the feed-forward units, the heads and the hidden coordinates; signs and powers of two) leave its
function unchanged, and gave a canonical form for the weights. This batch asks the practical question of that claim: take real checkpoints, apply the operations, write them as the files that people load, and run original and transformed in
several independent runtimes on test prompts. If the claim is right the transformation is invisible to every runtime, to within the noise of that runtime, in logits, text, perplexity, and speed; and the exceptions should be exactly where a runtime's
own storage format is not invariant to the operation. That second part is stated as a theorem below, so that the quantized runs are a test and not a survey.

The KV cache is in scope because it is the state that a runtime builds from the weights and then keeps: the user pointed to work on it by LARQL's author and by other researchers, and the batch asks what the symmetries do to it. Four research agents were
started on the prior art (the author's repositories and video, the structure and compression of the cache, how runtimes verify their cache paths, what the cache leaks); their reports had not been read when this was registered, no LARQL code, text or data is used here, and
what they contain will be declared as seen where it changes a later batch.

## What commutes with what (statements to be tested, with their proofs)

**Quantized weights.** A block quantizer stores, for each group of entries that share a scale, a scale computed from the group (the largest magnitude, or the minimum and maximum) and a small integer per entry, rounded from the entry divided by the scale. llama.cpp's Q8_0 and Q4_0 use groups of 32
consecutive entries along the input dimension of a row, and also quantize the activations of each matrix product in groups of 32 along the dimension that is summed; CTranslate2's int8 uses one scale per row of a weight and per row of an activation.
Take a transformation T of a weight matrix that maps entries to entries (a permutation of rows, of columns, or both) possibly with scalings.

**Claim 1.** If T maps every scale group onto a scale group, and every scaling it applies is the same power of two (or the same sign) on all entries of a group, then quantize(T W) = T quantize(W) exactly: the same integers, the same scales up to the power of two or the sign.
*Proof.* The scale is a function of the multiset of the group's entries that is equivariant to multiplication by 2^k (the maximum of magnitudes scales by 2^k; the signed maximum of Q4_0 changes sign with a uniform negation) and invariant to the order of
the entries; the integer of an entry is round(entry/scale), the same for T W's entry and W's entry when both are multiplied by the same 2^k (rounding to nearest commutes with multiplication by a power of two, and with negation if ties are rounded away from zero, which `roundf` does); and T moves groups to groups. Where
the scaling differs between the entries of a group, the group's scale is not the scaled scale and the integers change. Where T sends two entries of one group to different groups, the groups' scales and integers change. *End.*
(The product with a quantized activation is the same: the activation is quantized in groups along the summed dimension, and a transformation of the dimension that maps groups to groups moves the groups and nothing else; the dot product then adds the same block
sums in another order, which differs from the original by float rounding of the sum, and a rounding of the sum can move an activation across a rounding boundary of its own quantization: a difference of the size of one quantization step in one entry, which is why "the same" below is a ratio to the
quantization error and not zero.)

*Consequences* for the variants, for Q8_0 and Q4_0 of llama.cpp (blocks of 32 along each row): row operations on a matrix are always compatible (every row is its own set of groups), column operations are compatible only if they preserve the blocks of 32.
So `heads` is compatible (it permutes rows of q, k, v and moves the columns of o in runs of 64 = two blocks); `units_blk` and `resid_blk` are compatible by construction; `units` and `resid` (any permutation) and `scale` (a different power of two on each column of down and o) are **not** and will differ by the quantization noise
of two different roundings (measured on single real matrices by a research agent, with the real quantizers' code emulated: the quantized weights of a column-permuted matrix differ from the original's by about 1.4 times the quantization error itself). For CTranslate2's int8 (one scale per row, taken from the largest magnitude): every permutation of columns is compatible and every permutation of rows (the scale of a row is of its own entries), and scalings are compatible only on rows: so
`heads`, `units`, `resid`, `perm` are compatible and `scale` is not.

**Claim 2 (the cache).** The keys (after the rotary embedding) and values of a layer are functions of the layer's normalized input and of that layer's own input projections. Hence, in exact arithmetic: a signed permutation of the hidden coordinates, an orthogonal change of the stream with the norms folded, the
unit gauge and the norm gauge leave the cache unchanged (batch 0015's claims K1 to K3, proved there over a finite field for every nonlinearity); a permutation of the key/value groups permutes it (K4); the value/output gauge maps the values of a group by its matrix and leaves the keys (K5); the query/key gauge of rotary embeddings maps each key plane by its complex scalar and leaves the values (K6).
For the variants of this batch the maps are monomial: a permutation of the 64-channel groups and a diagonal of +-2^k.

**Claim 3 (the quantized cache).** llama.cpp stores the cache of each token as one row of n_kv x head_dim entries per layer, in blocks of 32 for Q8_0 and Q4_0 (head_dim is 64, so a group is two blocks). By Claim 1 and Claim 2: a quantized cache commutes with every variant that leaves the cache unchanged or permutes whole groups
(`resid`, `resid_blk`, `units`, `units_blk`, `heads`, `perm`), and does not commute with the variants that scale channels inside a block (`scale`, `all`, `canon`); an f16 cache commutes with powers of two as well (rounding to a float format commutes with multiplication by a power of two), so every exact variant commutes with it.

## How a run is judged

Rules, fixed before the runs and implemented in `grade14.py` (written before the results are read).

- **The reference.** `ref64.py` is an independent float64 implementation of the forward pass, run on the original in numpy; R64 is its logits on the 24 test sequences. **e(run)** is the NMSE of a run's logits against R64: the rounding error of that run. Control C0: the NMSE of PyTorch float32's original against R64 is at most 1e-9
  for each model (if not, the reference or PyTorch is wrong and nothing that is judged against R64 is graded). Hugging Face's own float64 run is not a reference: its RMSNorm, rotary tables and eager softmax compute in float32.
- **G0, comparable.** A float32 runtime is *comparable* on a model if e(original) <= 1e-7 and its top-1 agreement with R64 is at least 0.99. A runtime that is not comparable has its variants reported and not judged.
- **R1, invisible.** A variant is *invisible* in a (runtime, dtype) if its NMSE against the same runtime's own original is at most 4 max(e(original), e(variant)): the difference of two runs of one function is explained by their rounding errors (independent errors add; the factor 4 covers the worst case of opposite errors).
  *Bit identity* is stronger and is stated where it is predicted. "Exact variants" are `scale`, `perm`, `all`, `canon`, `heads`, `units`, `resid`, `units_blk`, `resid_blk`.
- **The ratio rule, for the quantized forms.** With e_q = e(the quantized original), a variant is *the same* if its NMSE against the quantized original is at most 0.2 e_q, and *different* if at least 0.5 e_q (two independent draws of the quantization noise differ by about 2 e_q; between 0.2 and 0.5 neither is claimed).
- **The margin rule, for generations.** A greedy generation that differs from the original's is *benign* if, at its first difference, the float64 top-1 minus top-2 logit of the original (along the runtime's own greedy generation of the original) is below 4 times the largest absolute logit difference of that variant against the runtime's original on the test set (a flip of the top-2 needs a margin below twice that difference).
- **Speed.** A variant's median throughput is within the original's own spread: between the minimum of the original's measurements less 3% and their maximum plus 3% (15 prefill and 9 decode measurements per original in the `models` jobs, 5 and 3 in `quant`).

## Predictions, with severity

Severity is how likely the prediction is to fail if the algebra of batch 0013 were wrong or the runtime did something unexpected with the files.

*Logits (cell 02).*

- **P1, moderate severity.** PyTorch, float32: for each of the four models, `scale` gives logits **bit for bit** those of the original (largest difference 0). (Observed once, on SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, from float32 arrays in batch 0013, and on the tiny random model in this batch's smoke; here the weights come from files, in the dtype of the original, and there are four models.)
- **P2, moderate severity.** PyTorch, float32: each of the other eight exact variants is invisible (R1) and has top-1 agreement at least 0.998 with the original, for each model.
- **P3, low-moderate severity.** PyTorch, bfloat16: `scale` is bit for bit the original; the other exact variants are invisible (R1). (The rounding of bfloat16 is the noise, and the bound is wide.)
- **P4, moderate severity.** ONNX Runtime, candle, llama.cpp (f32 GGUF with the f32 cache) and CTranslate2 (float32) are comparable (G0) for each of the four models. A runtime that is not is reported as such and its variants get no verdict.
- **P4b, low severity.** llama.cpp, SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, float32 weights: e with an f16 cache is at least 10 times e with the f32 cache. (Direction seen on the tiny random model: 9e-9 against 1e-14; not blind.)
- **P5a, high severity.** In every comparable (runtime, model): `scale` is bit for bit the runtime's own original. (Seen on the tiny random model: true in ONNX Runtime, candle and CTranslate2; not true in llama.cpp with its default f16 cache, NMSE 6e-19, which is why the cache is now f32; not blind.)
- **P5b, moderate severity.** In every comparable (runtime, model): every other exact variant is invisible (R1).
- **P6, high severity (the interaction with quantization, from Claim 1).** llama.cpp Q8_0 and Q4_0 of SmolLM2-135M-Instruct: `heads`, `units_blk` and `resid_blk` are *the same* as the quantized original (ratio rule); `units`, `resid`, `scale`, `perm`, `all` and `canon` are *different*. Refuted by a "same" variant above 0.2 e_q or a "different" variant below 0.5 e_q.
- **P7, moderate severity.** CTranslate2 int8 of SmolLM2-135M-Instruct: `heads`, `units`, `resid` and `perm` are *the same* as the int8 original; `scale`, `all` and `canon`, which scale columns, are *different*.

*Text (cell 03).*

- **P8, moderate severity.** PyTorch, float32: `scale` gives all generations identical to the original's (16 for the instruct model, 8 for the others); for every other exact variant every generation that differs is benign (margin rule).
- **P9, moderate severity.** The same in each comparable runtime in float32.
- **P10, high severity (the control).** `broken` in every runtime and dtype: perplexity at least 100 times the original's, top-1 agreement with the runtime's original at most 0.2, NMSE against it at least 0.01, and at most 1 generation identical to the original's.

*Perplexity (cell 04).*

- **P11, moderate severity.** In every comparable (runtime, model) in float32 and in PyTorch, every exact variant has a perplexity within 1e-5 (relative) of the original's.

*Speed (cell 05).*

- **P12, moderate severity.** In every runtime and float format, for each exact variant, the median prefill and decode throughput are within the original's own spread (judging rules), the peak resident memory is within 5% of the original's, and the sizes of the files of a (runtime, dtype) are the same for every variant stored in one dtype.

*Tokenizers (cell 07).*

- **P13, low-moderate severity.** llama.cpp's tokenizer gives Hugging Face's ids on all 33 texts for each of the four models. (On the tiny random model, whose tokenizer is a different kind, 32 of 33 agreed; not blind about the cell, blind about these models.)

*The KV cache (cell 08).*

- **P14, high severity.** For each of the four models, in both views (float64 reference; Transformers float32), for every layer, keys and values both: the cache of `resid`, `resid_blk`, `units` and `units_blk` is the original's (the matching is the identity, every factor 1); of `perm` and `heads`, a permutation of the groups (a bijection, every factor 1); of `scale`, `all` and `canon`, a bijection with every factor +-2^k. The residual of each fit is at most the tolerance of the view.
- **P14c, control.** For `broken` the cache is not a monomial map of the original's in at least one layer (it is wrong from layer 0).

*The type of the cache in llama.cpp (cell 09).*

- **P15, high severity (from Claims 1 to 3).** llama.cpp, SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, float32 weights: with an f16 cache every exact variant is *the same* as the original (ratio rule with e = e(original with that cache)); with a Q8_0 cache and with a Q4_0 cache, `heads`, `perm`, `resid`, `resid_blk`, `units` and `units_blk` are *the same* and `scale`, `all` and `canon` are *different*.

*Instrument controls.* C0 (the reference against PyTorch, above); the original run twice in one runtime and dtype gives bit-identical logits, equal perplexity and equal generations; every tensor of every variant has the original's multiset of significands and the original's name; the config keys that the writer changed are only the dtype keys and negative token ids; the safetensors files of the variants are pairwise different, and so are the converted artifacts of one runtime and dtype (a stale conversion would repeat one);
the manifest records `scale`, `perm`, `all` and `canon` of SmolLM2 as stored in bfloat16 with no tensor outside the grid; every run of every variant of every model is accepted by the architecture gate; for the float16 Spectra models any variant that does not fit is reported with its dtype.

*Observed, no prediction.* e of each runtime and dtype; the cache-type errors e(f16), e(Q8_0), e(Q4_0); the quantization errors e_q of the weights; the speed of each runtime relative to the others; the size of the quantized files; Q4_0 and Q8_0 perplexities; which variants a converter refused; the factors of the cache fits (the range of the powers of two).

## Rivals to exclude

- *A runtime that disagrees with the float64 reference on the original* is not evidence about the variants; it is reported as not comparable and its variant numbers are shown without a verdict.
- *A variant that is exact only because the runtime reads weights from a cache of the original.* The directories differ in content (the hashes of the files and of the converted artifacts are kept) and the converters are run per variant.
- *Quantization noise taken for a symmetry failure.* The quantized cells compare with the same runtime's quantized original, in units of its quantization error, and the claims above say which variants should agree; those that should differ are predicted to differ.
- *A cache difference taken for a transformation that does not commute with the cache type.* The cache type is varied in its own cell; every other cell uses a float cache.

## Addendum 1 (2026-10-06, written while the heavy run was in progress and before any SmolLM2 number of cells 08 and 09 was read)

**What was found.** After the registration, a research agent's notes on the structure of the KV cache said that llama.cpp rotates quantized caches, and I read the source of the pinned tag (b11450, `src/llama-kv-cache.cpp` lines 315 to 353, `src/llama-graph.cpp` around lines 2959 to 3004): when the type of the K cache (or of the V cache) is quantized and the head dimension is a multiple of 64, the keys (after the rotary embedding) and the queries are multiplied by an orthonormal Walsh-Hadamard matrix, 64 wide (doubled while the head dimension is a multiple of twice its width), per head, before the cache is written, and the attention output of the values is rotated back; f32, f16 and bf16 caches are never rotated; `LLAMA_ATTN_ROT_DISABLE=1` turns it off.

**What it does to the registered text.** Claim 3 derives the commutation of a quantized cache from blocks of 32 along the head dimension, which is the mechanism of an unrotated cache. In the default quantized caches of this build the stored values are the Hadamard-rotated ones, so the mechanism that the claim names is not what runs. The classification that P15 predicts (the same for `heads`, `perm`, `resid`, `resid_blk`, `units`, `units_blk`: the cache is unchanged or whole heads move, and the rotation is the same for every head; different for `scale`, `all`, `canon`: the stored vectors change) may still hold, by the generic argument that two different vectors are two different draws of the quantization noise. P15 is graded as registered. Cells 08 (the cache in float) and the f16 half of P15 are not affected by the rotation.

**What is added** (the code will be committed after the heavy run has finished, so as not to cancel it, and the predictions below are fixed now):

- **Cell 09b.** The same runs as cell 09 for the Q8_0 and the Q4_0 cache with `LLAMA_ATTN_ROT_DISABLE=1` (a dtype suffix `-norot`, for example `float32-kvq4_0-norot`), original and every variant, SmolLM2-135M-Instruct.
- **P15n, high severity.** With the rotation disabled, Claim 3 holds as derived: for the Q8_0 and for the Q4_0 cache, `heads`, `perm`, `resid`, `resid_blk`, `units` and `units_blk` are *the same* as the original with that cache, and `scale`, `all` and `canon` are *different* (the ratio rule, with e = e(original with that cache, rotation disabled)).
- **P15r, moderate severity.** The rotation lowers the error of the quantized cache: e(Q4_0 cache, rotated) is at most 0.8 times e(Q4_0 cache, not rotated), and e(Q8_0 cache, rotated) is at most 1.0 times e(Q8_0 cache, not rotated). (A research note reports the same direction for Qwen3-0.6B-Base: perplexity 62.0 to 46.3 for Q4_0; not blind about the direction.)

A refutation of P15 together with the confirmation of P15n would mean that the rotated cache commutes with different variants than the unrotated one; the converse would mean that the generic argument is enough.

Two things in the data qualify the refutation. ## Addendum 2 (2026-10-07, written after the results above were read: not blind about the direction of what it tests)

The refutations of P6 (Q8_0 weights) and P15 (f16 and Q8_0 cache) have two explanations that this batch's data cannot separate: (H) the variants that the claims say commute do commute, and a run with a fine quantizer amplifies the float32 noise that any change of the order of the sums causes into a difference of about a half of that quantizer's own error; (H') they do not commute with the fine quantizer in the way Claims 1 and 3 derived, for a reason the derivation missed.
