## Purpose

The user's instruction: develop the special theory of transformer language models, evidenced by specific systems of language models, as a first step toward a generally covariant version; test a covariant optimizer; run the challenges of the previous review in CI. Three corrections of the user shaped what follows, and are recorded because they change the claims:

1. *A function that is called a language model is not thereby one* ("'Snow is white' if and only if snow is in fact white"). An earlier version of this batch analysed a random-constant program of 232 numbers under names such as "tiny model" and "system". It is not a language model, so nothing proved about it is evidence about language models. It is now called the **finite instance**, defined in the theory document, and every claim names its **subject**. Claims about language models are graded on checkpoints that loads in Transformers, were fitted to text, and pass a registered test that can fail (below).
2. *The correspondence with special and general relativity is between mathematical structures*, not a claim that the machines are unphysical. The machines enter as the instrument (§6 of the theory document).
3. *Words must say what they mean.* The theory document defines each term once, in the vocabulary of formal systems and programming of the twentieth century, and uses no other.

## The registered test of "language model"

A checkpoint is counted as a language model in the tables of this batch if the mean next-token loss of the checkpoint, in float64, on the 100-word English text `batches/0023/membership.txt` (written for this batch; the same for all checkpoints; at most 256 tokens) is at most 0.9 ln(V), V the size of its output vocabulary. The loss of a function that knows nothing is ln V. The claims S0, Q0, G0, P0 grade it for the language models; the finite instance, fed random strings of its alphabet, is the control (it should score about 1 and be rejected). The text is simple children's English because one checkpoint of the batch (delphi-suite/v0-llama2-100k) is a small model whose training data the page I read does not state; if it fails the test it is not a language model by this definition and the table says so.

## How the claims are graded, and what is counted

`grade23.py` reads measurements and applies the thresholds that are in it; the experiments write no verdict. A claim is **as predicted**, **REFUTED**, or **not run** (a file or number it needs is missing, or its validity condition failed: nothing is concluded). A NaN never satisfies a threshold. **Controls** are listed apart and are never counted as claims: checks that an experiment can fail (the rotation without the fold must break; a shift must move the cache; the variant with the permutation applied to the layers but not the embedding must give other tokens; the finite instance must fail the language-model test) and that the harness works. `grade23.py --selftest`, run first in CI, builds a fixture that satisfies every claim, then for each claim violates each of its own inputs one at a time and requires REFUTED, and requires "not run" on missing data: 119 claims and 19 controls pass it. The job `grade` fails if any claim is not "as predicted" or any control fails; a refuted claim therefore makes CI red, on purpose.

## Known weaknesses of this registration

- The thresholds and counts are mine. Where a threshold comes from a number seen before (pilots 1 to 4 above), the pilot is listed; the kinematic, outside-the-class, conversation and fine-tuning claims were not piloted at all, because the code that makes them has not run locally (it needs PyTorch, which this machine cannot hold); the smoke job runs it in CI on small random models of each architecture, ungraded, before the registered run, and its faults are declared.
- The mutants were chosen by me, by hand, among changes whose text I could target; they are not the operators of a mutation-testing tool and the kill rate depends on the choice. The 8 predicted survivors are my guesses at what the Lean–Python comparison cannot see at one rotary plane, two positions, an untied head, the RMS norm and the causal mask.
- The conformance comparison is with the **stored** output of the Lean definition (batch 0022's CI runs); Lean is not run again.
- Draws, prompts and transformations are one set each. The runs are on GitHub-hosted machines of one kind; nobody else has re-run them.
- The test of "language model" is a necessary condition (the loss is below chance on one English text), not a definition of a working language model; it can reject a random function and it cannot show that a checkpoint converses.
- The theory document contains derivations by hand (the behaviour of Qwen3's normalization, of LayerNorm) that are in neither the Lean development nor the Datalog program; the claims Q2–Q7, G1–G3, P3–P5 test them, and a refutation is a result.
- The finite instance's claims are claims about the scheme's equations. They are not claims about language models and are not counted as such.

**2. **3. Position, in the Llama family and the systems that have a rotary table (K1–K5, Q1, Q7, Q8, P1, G4).** Shifting every position by 1, 16 or 128 changes the scores by at most 1.1e-14 (relative) in all seven systems that have the table (five Llama-family checkpoints, Qwen3, Pythia), in float64 with the float32 islands of Transformers replaced (K1, Q1, P1). In the Llama family the same shift is a change of the weights: rotating each rotary plane of the queries and keys by 16 θ_p leaves the scores unchanged (at most 5e-15) and the keys that the cache holds after the rotary embedding equal the original's at the shifted positions (at most 3e-15) (K2). In Qwen3, which normalizes the queries and the keys before the rotary embedding, the same weight change is not an equivalence: it changes the scores by 0.48 to 0.72 (Q2, Q7). GPT-2, with learned absolute positions, changes by 0.36 to 0.69 when the positions are shifted by 1 or 16, as it must (G4). Dilating the angles changes the scores by at least 0.016 in each of the five Llama-family systems (0.016, 0.087, 0.096, 0.10, 0.17 at the smallest of each), by 0.18 to 0.51 in Qwen3 (K3, Q8), and by 9.5e-4 to 4.6e-3 in Pythia (P2, refuted). With the norm weights folded and the head untied, rotating the stream by a random orthogonal matrix leaves the scores unchanged (at most 4.1e-14 in the Llama family, 2.0e-14 in Qwen3), the stream after the embedding and after each layer equals the original times Qᵀ, its Gram matrix is unchanged in every layer, and the same rotation without the fold changes the scores by 0.24 to 1.7 (K5, Q9; the control).

**4. LayerNorm checkpoints (G1–G3, P3–P5).** Before this batch LayerNorm had been tested only in a toy model with random constants (batch 0015); this is its first test on trained checkpoints. In GPT-2: adding the all-ones vector to the outputs of the output projections of every block (G1, at most 2.7e-15), the dual shift of the readers of the two LayerNorms (G2, at most 1.2e-14), and a constant added to the embedding rows of five tokens (G3: the other scores are unchanged exactly, the five change by the predicted amount to 9e-16) all leave the function as it should be. In Pythia-160M: the same shifts (P3, at most 1.6e-15) and an orthogonal rotation that fixes the all-ones vector, with the LayerNorms folded (P4, at most 2.6e-15), leave the scores unchanged; a general orthogonal rotation changes them by 1.6e-3 to 2.1e-3 (P5), as the derivation says it must (the all-ones vector is what the LayerNorm removes).

**5. The norm of the queries and keys (Q3–Q6, Qwen3-0.6B).** A scalar pair on the keys and queries of a group and plane (T1) changes the scores by 0.40 to 0.62 and a rotation of a plane (T2) by 0.60 to 1.0: with the norm they are not equivalences. A positive scalar on the query projection of a head (T3) changes them by 2.1e-6 to 7.0e-6 (exact up to the epsilon of the norm; at most 1e-4 was predicted). Moving a scalar per plane from the gain of the query norm to the gain of the key norm (T4) leaves them unchanged (at most 1.1e-14). This is the one new equivalence transformation that the QK norm adds, and it was predicted.

**6. A conversation and a tool call (C).** Qwen3-0.6B, in float64 with greedy decoding for 64 tokens, given its own chat template with one tool, `get_weather(city)`, and the request "What is the weather in Paris right now? Use the tool.", answers
`<tool_call>\n{"name": "get_weather", "arguments": {"city": "Paris"}}\n</tool_call>` and ends its turn. Each of eight transformed copies of its weights (a signed permutation of the hidden coordinates; the units of the feed-forward blocks permuted and scaled by ±2^k; an invertible matrix on the value dimensions; two heads exchanged; the stream rotated after the norm weights are folded and the head untied; the QK-norm transformations T3 and T4; and the composition of all of them) gives **the same 64 tokens, the same tool call**. The control, in which the permutation is applied to the layers and not to the embedding, produces noise from the first token. The same for "Say hello in one short sentence." (answer: "Hello! How can I assist you today?", 8 of 8 copies) and, for SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, for its six copies ("Hello!").

**7. Fine-tuning (H).** Ten steps on one fixed text, from the original and from copies that are the same function, in float64 with PyTorch's own optimizers; the difference of the two functions after training is given as a multiple of how far the original run moved the function.

| system, optimizer | loss, first → last | signed permutation | rotation of the stream | scaling of the units | invertible matrix on the value dimensions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, SGD (lr 0.05) | 3.50 → 18.35 | 7e-12 | 8e-10 | 0.54 | 0.93 |
| SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, AdamW (lr 2e-5) | 3.50 → 0.94 | 2e-14 | **1.6** | 0.46 | 0.13 |
| delphi 100k, SGD | 6.76 → 9.43 | 5e-12 | 7e-12 | 0.99 | 1.1 |
| delphi 100k, AdamW | 6.76 → 4.65 | 9e-15 | **0.098** | 0.35 | 0.073 |

As predicted (all twelve entries of H): gradient descent keeps the orthogonal transformations and loses the two others; AdamW keeps only the signed permutation, and a rotation of the stream, which is a symmetry of the function, gives a different trained function (for SmolLM2 the two functions are further apart than the original run moved). **A limit of this test that I must state: gradient descent at the registered learning rate 0.05 did not train; it raised the loss of both models (3.5 → 18.4 and 6.8 → 9.4).** The test of equivariance is still valid (the transformed runs follow the original to 1e-9 through a divergent trajectory, which is a stricter test, and part company at once for the other two), but the numbers are not those of a useful fine-tuning, and I chose that learning rate from a pilot on another model.

**8. A covariant optimizer (O1–O8): the answer to "test one".** The question: which training rules define a motion of the *function* and not only of the constants, so that two constant vectors that are the same function are carried to the same function? In continuous time that holds if and only if the velocity that the rule gives to the function is the same at the two points. Measured at ten random points of the finite instance (232 constants; the equations of the scheme, not a language model) under seven equivalence transformations g, as the largest relative difference of the velocities of the predictive distributions at θ and at gθ:

| rule | unit permutation | signed permutation | head swap | rotation of the stream | unit scaling ±2^k | invertible matrix on the value dimensions | complex scalar on a rotary plane | median norm of the step in the constants, over that of gradient descent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gradient descent | 4.61e-16 | 1.97e-15 | 3.01e-15 | 2.37e-14 | 1.41e+00 | 2.92e-01 | 1.44e-02 | 1 |
| Adam (first step) | 4.36e-16 | 1.42e-15 | 1.69e-15 | 4.52e-01 | 4.08e-01 | 2.52e-01 | 5.31e-02 | 23.8 |
| natural gradient, pseudo-inverse | 2.05e-10 | 1.86e-10 | 1.67e-10 | 1.74e-10 | 2.47e-10 | 2.15e-10 | 2.42e-10 | 6.97e+04 |
| natural gradient, damped by the identity of the constants (F + 1e-9 I) | 2.56e-12 | 2.01e-12 | 2.09e-12 | 2.74e-12 | 4.02e-02 | 6.65e-04 | 2.89e-03 | 983 |
| natural gradient, damped by the metric of the scores (F + λ JᵀJ, λ = 1e-6 times the mean nonzero eigenvalue of F) | 2.23e-10 | 2.50e-10 | 2.24e-10 | 2.56e-10 | 1.82e-10 | 2.44e-10 | 2.07e-10 | 6.97e+04 |
| natural gradient, damped by the metric of the scores (F + λ JᵀJ, λ = 1e-2 times the mean nonzero eigenvalue of F) | 1.34e-10 | 1.92e-10 | 1.84e-10 | 1.97e-10 | 1.52e-10 | 1.66e-10 | 1.85e-10 | 6.66e+04 |

(The entries near 1e-15 are float64 rounding, those near 1e-10 the cut-off of the pseudo-inverse; the entries of 1e-4 and above are real differences of velocity.) So: **natural gradient is covariant under all seven transformations; gradient descent under the four that are orthogonal on the constants; Adam under the permutations and signs only.** Damping the natural gradient by a fixed multiple of the identity of the constants brings the Euclidean metric of the constants back as an absolute object and the covariance is lost for the three non-orthogonal transformations; damping it by the metric of the scores, which is a metric on functions, keeps it. Over 200 steps from θ and from gθ the same pattern holds for the trajectories themselves: gradient descent stays within 6e-12 of the other trajectory for the four orthogonal transformations and parts by 1.4 to 2.4 for the other three; Adam stays within 7e-11 for the unit permutation, the signed permutation and the head swap and parts by 1.0 to 2.0 for the others (O7, O8). The price is in the constants: the covariant step is **70,000 times** as long as the gradient-descent step (median over the points), because the function moves along directions in which the constants must move far. I tried the discrete-time covariant update that would normally be used, a proximal step in the function space (minimize L + KL/η), and its inner problem did not converge at this size; that negative result is in the pilots, and **what is established here is a velocity field, not an update rule that anyone could use for training**. And it is at one size, 232 constants, on the equations and not on a language model.

**9. The finite instance as a checkpoint, in three runtimes (B1–B3).** Written as a Hugging Face checkpoint and run on every one of its 625 sequences of four symbols (780 contexts): Transformers in float64 against the numpy reference, 2e-16 in relative norm; in float32, 1.2e-7; candle (Rust, float32), 1.3e-7; the largest score is the same at every position in all three (agreement 1.0). The two float32 runtimes agree with the reference to float32 rounding on all of the inputs: this is a conformance statement about the equations at one size, and says nothing about language models.

**10. The check that the Lean–Python conformance run is able to fail (M).** The Lean definitions of batches 0020 to 0022 were checked against the Python definition on random instances over a prime field (four configurations, 278 arrays each). To learn what that check can see, 41 mutants were made (one-line changes of the Python definition, of its surrogates, of the transformations and of the generator) and each was run through the same comparison with the stored Lean output: the unmutated copy agrees on all 278 arrays in every configuration; **33 of the 33 mutants that were predicted to be detected are detected** (32 of them in all four configurations, G08 in two); **the 8 that were predicted to survive survive**, and they are the check's blind spots: the pairing convention of a rotary plane (with only one plane in the configurations: M07, G12), the direction of the scale on the final norm (the generator takes the scale 1 there: G11), the recurrence of the rotation beyond two positions (H02), and four features that the configurations do not contain (a tied head, LayerNorm, a non-causal mask, the untying in the fold of an already untied head: B01–B04). The conformance of the Lean definition with the Python one therefore does not cover those, and the verification suite says so.

**What these results do not show.** That the list of equivalence transformations is complete: the proofs are of soundness, the rank counts are at generic points of the continuous part, and the discrete part is counted for three families only. Anything about mixtures of experts, latent attention, sliding windows, biases, or sandwich norms. Anything about the finite instance as a language model. That the covariant velocity can be turned into a usable training step. That the results hold for the same checkpoints in float32 or in a quantized runtime (the instrument of §6 of the theory; batches 0014 and 0024). Anything physical about the machines that run these models: the mathematical structure is compared with the relativity models, and the machines are the instrument.
