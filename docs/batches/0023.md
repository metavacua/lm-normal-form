## Purpose

The user's instruction: develop the special theory of transformer language models, evidenced by specific systems of language models; test a covariant optimizer; run the challenges of the previous review in CI. Three corrections of the user shaped what follows, and are recorded because they change the claims:

1. *A function that is called a language model is not thereby one* ("'Snow is white' if and only if snow is in fact white"). An earlier version of this batch analysed a random-constant program of 232 numbers under names such as "tiny model" and "system". It is not a language model, so nothing proved about it is evidence about language models. It is now called the **finite instance**, defined in the theory document, and every claim names its **subject**. Claims about language models are graded on checkpoints that load in Transformers, were fitted to text, and pass the registered inclusion test (below).
2. *The correspondence with special and general relativity is between mathematical structures*, not a claim that the machines are unphysical. The machines enter as the instrument (§6 of the theory document).
3. *Words must say what they mean.* The theory document defines each term once, in the vocabulary of formal systems and programming of the twentieth century, and uses no other.

## The registered inclusion test of "language model"

A checkpoint is counted as a language model in the tables of this batch if the mean next-token loss of the checkpoint, in float64, on the 100-word English text `batches/0023/membership.txt` (written for this batch; the same for all checkpoints; at most 256 tokens) is at most 0.9 ln(V), V the size of its output vocabulary. The loss of a function that knows nothing is ln V. The test decides which checkpoints the claims are about; it is a labelling rule and not a claim, and `grade23.py` lists it apart (8 checkpoints, 8 pass). The text is simple children's English because one checkpoint of the batch (delphi-suite/v0-llama2-100k) is a small model whose training data the page I read does not state.

## How the claims are graded, and what is counted

`grade23.py` reads measurements and applies the thresholds that are in it; the experiments write no verdict. A claim is **as predicted**, **REFUTED**, or **not run** (a file or number it needs is missing, or its validity condition failed: nothing is concluded). A NaN never satisfies a threshold. **Controls** are listed apart and are never counted as claims: checks that an experiment can fail (the rotation without the fold must break; a shift must move the cache; the variant with the permutation applied to the layers but not the embedding must give other tokens). The **inclusion test** of the checkpoints is listed apart too. `grade23.py --selftest`, run first in CI, builds a fixture that satisfies every claim, then for each claim violates each of its own inputs one at a time and requires REFUTED, and requires "not run" on missing data: 102 claims, 13 controls and 8 inclusion checks pass it. The job `grade` fails if any claim is not "as predicted", any control fails or any checkpoint fails the inclusion test; a refuted claim therefore makes CI red, on purpose.

## Limits of this registration

- The thresholds are mine and not derived; the counts (26, 28, 62, 16, 384, 48, 8) are derived and checked. The 12 claims of the form "changes the logits by at least 1e-3" are decided by that one threshold; two of them have measured values within a factor 2 of it (P2: 9.5e-4; P5: 1.6e-3).
- The mutants were chosen by me, by hand, among changes whose text I could target; they are not the operators of a mutation-testing tool.
- The conformance comparison is with the **stored** output of the Lean definition (batch 0022's CI runs); Lean is not run again.
- Draws, prompts and transformations are one set each. The runs are on GitHub-hosted machines of one kind; nobody else has re-run them.
- The inclusion test is a necessary condition (the loss is below chance on one English text), not a definition of a working language model.
- The theory document contains derivations by hand (the behaviour of Qwen3's normalization, of LayerNorm) that are in neither the Lean development nor the Datalog program; the claims Q2–Q6, G1–G3, P3–P5 test them, and a refutation is a result.

**2. The group (R1–R6, F1, D1–D3).** For the finite instance, at five random draws: the rank deficiency of the Jacobian of the function with respect to the 232 constants is 26 in every draw, with a gap of at least 6e9 between the last singular value counted as non-zero and the first counted as zero (R1). With the frequencies of the rotary planes made constants of the scheme it is still 26 (R2a); with the position of token t made a function of the input (a proper time) and the frequencies fixed it is 26 (R2b); with both free it is **28** (R2c): the one new transformation per layer, b → b + s, θ → θ e^−s. With a projection of the queries and keys of its own at every position (no rotary table) the deficiency is 62 = 30 + 2 × 16 (R3), and at a point of that larger model that is the image of a point of the rotary model the two functions are equal to 1e-15 and the larger model's fibre has dimension 62 (R4). The extra directions that a vocabulary no larger than the hidden size gives were as the rule said (extra 10 at (d, V) = (4, 3), 2 at (6, 6); none at (5, 6) and (4, 8); R5a, R5b; the same value at each of the three draws of a configuration, so four distinct data points). **3. Position, in the Llama family and the systems that have a rotary table (K1–K5, Q1, Q8, Q9, P1, G4).** Shifting every position by 1, 16 or 128 changes the scores by at most 1.1e-14 (relative) in all seven systems that have the table (five Llama-family checkpoints, Qwen3, Pythia), in float64 with the float32 islands of Transformers replaced (K1, Q1, P1). In the Llama family the same shift is a change of the weights: rotating each rotary plane of the queries and keys by 16 θ_p leaves the scores unchanged (at most 5e-15) and the keys that the cache holds after the rotary embedding equal the original's at the shifted positions (at most 3e-15) (K2). In Qwen3, which normalizes the queries and the keys before the rotary embedding, the same weight change is not an equivalence: it changes the scores by 0.48 to 0.72 (Q2). GPT-2, with learned absolute positions, changes by 0.36 to 0.69 when the positions are shifted by 1 or 16, as it must (G4). Dilating the angles changes the scores by at least 0.016 in each of the five Llama-family systems (0.016, 0.087, 0.096, 0.10, 0.17 at the smallest of each), by 0.18 to 0.51 in Qwen3 (K3, Q8), and by 9.5e-4 to 4.6e-3 in Pythia (P2, refuted). With the norm weights folded and the head untied, rotating the stream by a random orthogonal matrix leaves the scores unchanged (at most 4.1e-14 in the Llama family, 2.0e-14 in Qwen3), the stream after the embedding and after each layer equals the original times Qᵀ, its Gram matrix is unchanged in every layer, and the same rotation without the fold changes the scores by 0.24 to 1.7 (K5, Q9; the control).

**4. LayerNorm checkpoints (G1–G3, P3–P5).** Before this batch LayerNorm had been tested only in a toy model with random constants (batch 0015); this is its first test on trained checkpoints. In GPT-2: adding the all-ones vector to the outputs of the output projections of every block (G1, at most 2.7e-15) and the dual shift of the readers of the two LayerNorms (G2, at most 1.2e-14) leave the function unchanged. G3 adds a constant to the embedding rows of five tokens drawn at random from the vocabulary: the five scores of those tokens change by the predicted amount (c_v times the sum of the final LayerNorm's output) to 9e-16, and the other scores by exactly 0. The exact 0 is what results when none of the five tokens occurs in the six sentences, which was not recorded; G3 therefore tests the shifted rows of the tied head, and an occurrence of a shifted token in the input is not tested. In Pythia-160M: the same shifts (P3, at most 1.6e-15) and an orthogonal rotation that fixes the all-ones vector, with the LayerNorms folded (P4, at most 2.6e-15), leave the scores unchanged; a general orthogonal rotation changes them by 1.6e-3 to 2.1e-3 (P5), as the derivation says it must (the all-ones vector is what the LayerNorm removes).

**5. The norm of the queries and keys (Q3–Q6, Qwen3-0.6B).** A scalar pair on the keys and queries of a group and plane (T1) changes the scores by 0.40 to 0.62 and a rotation of a plane (T2) by 0.60 to 1.0: with the norm they are not equivalences. A positive scalar on the query projection of a head (T3) changes them by 2.1e-6 to 7.0e-6 (exact up to the epsilon of the norm; at most 1e-4 was predicted). Moving a scalar per plane from the gain of the query norm to the gain of the key norm (T4) leaves them unchanged (at most 1.1e-14). This is the one new equivalence transformation that the QK norm adds, and it was predicted.

A conversation and a tool call (C).** Qwen3-0.6B, in float64 with greedy decoding (at most 64 new tokens, ending at the end-of-turn token `<|im_end|>`), given its own chat template with one tool, `get_weather(city)`, and the request "What is the weather in Paris right now? Use the tool.", answers

**8. Training rules and equivariance (O1–O8).** The question: which training rules define a motion of the *function* and not only of the constants, so that two constant vectors that are the same function are carried to the same function? In continuous time that holds if and only if the velocity that the rule gives to the function is the same at the two points. Measured at ten random points of the finite instance (232 constants; the equations of the scheme, not a language model) under seven equivalence transformations g, as the largest relative difference of the velocities of the predictive distributions at θ and at gθ ("Adam" is its first step, which is sign descent):

So: **natural gradient is covariant under all seven transformations; gradient descent under the four that are orthogonal on the constants; Adam under the permutations and signs only.** O1, O2, O3 and O5 check the implementation against equivariances that hold by definition or are standard (gradient flow under isometries; the function-space velocity of natural gradient depends only on the function); O4 (the identity damping breaks it: the metric of the constants returns as an absolute object for the three non-orthogonal transformations; damping by the metric of the scores, which is a metric on functions, keeps it) and O7, O8 are the new content. Over 200 steps from θ and from gθ the same pattern holds for the trajectories themselves: gradient descent stays within 6e-12 of the other trajectory for the four orthogonal transformations and parts by 1.4 to 2.4 for the other three; Adam stays within 7e-11 for the unit permutation, the signed permutation and the head swap and parts by 1.0 to 2.0 for the others (O7, O8). The discrete-time covariant update that would normally be used, a proximal step in the function space (minimize L + KL/η), did not converge in its inner problem at this size and is not used; **what is established here is a velocity field, not an update rule that anyone could use for training**, at one size, 232 constants, on the equations and not on a language model.

**9. The finite instance as a checkpoint, in three runtimes (B1–B3).** Written as a Hugging Face checkpoint and run on every one of its 625 sequences of four symbols (780 contexts): Transformers in float64 against the numpy reference, 2e-16 in relative norm; in float32, 1.2e-7; candle (Rust, float32), 1.3e-7; the largest score is the same at every position in all three (agreement 1.0). The two float32 runtimes agree with the reference to float32 rounding on all of the inputs: this is a conformance statement about the equations at one size, and says nothing about language models.

**10. The check that the Lean–Python conformance run is able to fail (M).** The Lean definitions of batches 0020 to 0022 were checked against the Python definition on random instances over a prime field (four configurations, 278 arrays each). To learn what that check can see, 41 mutants were made (one-line changes of the Python definition, of its surrogates, of the transformations and of the generator) and each was run through the same comparison with the stored Lean output: the unmutated copy agrees on all 278 arrays in every configuration. **33 mutants are detected** (all of them in all four configurations except G08, in two; 32 by a mismatch of the arrays and M04 by an error of the generator, "no seed makes the parameters generic"); these 33 are the claims. **The other 8 are not detected, and are not claims** because the four configurations do not reach the changed code or the change is equivalent there: the pairing convention of a rotary plane (one plane in the configurations: M07, G12), the direction of the scale on the final norm (the generator takes the scale 1 there: G11), the recurrence of the rotation beyond two positions (H02), and four features that the configurations do not contain (a tied head, LayerNorm, a non-causal mask, the untying in the fold of an already untied head: B01–B04). The conformance of the Lean definition with the Python one therefore does not cover those, and the verification suite says so.

**What these results do not show.** That the list of equivalence transformations is complete: the proofs are of soundness, the rank counts are at generic points of the continuous part, and the discrete part is counted for three families only. Anything about mixtures of experts, latent attention, sliding windows, biases, or sandwich norms. Anything about the finite instance as a language model. That the covariant velocity can be turned into a usable training step. That the results hold for the same checkpoints in float32 or in a quantized runtime (the instrument of §6 of the theory; batches 0014 and 0024). Anything physical about the machines that run these models: the mathematical structure is compared with the relativity models, and the machines are the instrument.
