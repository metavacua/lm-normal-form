# Batch 0004: integrity of one stored relation on a finite domain

- **Registered:** 2026-10-06, before any cell ran.
- **Status:** not yet run. Results are appended below once CI has produced them.
- **Branch:** `batch/0004-integrity`, on top of batch 0003.
  **Workflow:** `.github/workflows/batch-0004.yml`. **Data:** `batches/0004/`.
  **Queries:** `queries/0004/`.

## Purpose

If a language model is a database, its quality is integrity, not a benchmark
score. This batch measures the integrity of one relation, *capital of*, on
the finite domain of sovereign states, against a named authority at a
recorded time, for twelve models. It separates what is checked by logic
alone from what is checked against the authority, and it separates the
model's capability for fiction from its tendency to it.

## The pair (L, T)

- **T, the authority:** Wikidata, the capital (P36) of every sovereign state
  not dissolved, fetched at the start of the run; the time of the fetch is
  recorded. The **canonical** capital of a country is the one Wikidata
  itself prefers (its "truthy" statement). A country is **determined** when
  it has exactly one canonical capital with an English label; only
  determined countries are prompted. Every capital a country ever had is
  kept as well, with rank and validity, so that an answer can be canonical,
  historical, another country's capital, or no capital at all.
- **L, the logic:** the reflexivity of identity, read as a schema over
  terms, and persistence of a truth under extension of its context. Neither
  needs the authority.
- **Values:** every readout is a next-token distribution, the model's
  softmax over its vocabulary, so a number here is the model's mass on a
  token and nothing else until calibration says otherwise.

## Readouts

Each model answers about 1,800 prompts, one next-token distribution each,
with the twenty most probable tokens kept. Nine kinds per determined
country X with canonical capital C, paired with the next country Y in
alphabetical order, whose capital is CY:

- **fwd:** "The capital of X is" → ` C`.
- **inv:** "C is the capital of" → ` X`.
- **ident:** "The capital of X is the capital of" → ` X`.
- **persist:** "The capital of Y is CY. The capital of X is" → ` C`.
- **raw_true:** "The capital of X is C. The capital of X is" → ` C`.
- **story_in:** "In the story, the capital of X is CY. In the story, the
  capital of X is" → ` CY`.
- **story_out:** "In the story, the capital of X is CY. Outside the story,
  the capital of X is" → ` C`; ` CY` here is collapse.
- **affirm:** "True or false: the capital of X is the capital of X. Answer:"
  → ` True`.
- **affirm_ctrl:** "True or false: the capital of X is the capital of Y.
  Answer:" → ` False`.

And for twelve invented country names: **invented_fwd** (no target; a gap
is the right answer) and **invented_ident** (the name itself).

A first-ranked token counts as the target when it begins the target and is
longer than one character. Matching is by string prefix, so it does not
depend on any tokenizer.

## Models

- SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, SmolLM2-360M-Instruct, SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct, and
  SmolLM2-135M without instruction tuning.
- Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B.
- TriLM 99M, 390M and 1.1B: ternary weights, Apache-2.0.
- GPT-2 and GPT-2 medium.

Ten are read through llama.cpp, after conversion by its own converter
(float32, or float16 for Qwen3-4B and the TriLMs, whose ternary values
float16 holds exactly). GPT-2's checkpoints are refused by that converter
(batch 0002), so the two GPT-2 cells are read through PyTorch by one
invocation that loops over the prompt file. Batch 0003 showed the two
readouts agree to four decimal places.

## What the judge computes

In Oxigraph, over the authority graph, the prompt graph and every model's
answers:

- **hits:** per model and kind, how often the first-ranked token is the
  target, and the mean mass on the target.
- **reflexivity:** per model, the countries for which the forward and the
  inverse readouts both return (both ways), one does (forward only, inverse
  only), or neither.
- **soundness:** per model, forward answers that are canonical, historical,
  another country's capital (a glut), or no capital (a gap).
- **persistence:** per model, how many countries lose more than 0.1 of mass
  on the canonical capital when an irrelevant true sentence precedes the
  prompt.
- **story:** per model, amnesia (the story's capital not returned inside the
  story) and collapse (the story's capital returned outside it).
- **invented:** per model, the mean confidence of the first-ranked token for
  invented countries and how often it is a real capital.
- **calibration:** per model, mass on the canonical capital in bins against
  the hit rate.
- **top1:** every first-ranked answer, for the record.

## Expected

- **Forward soundness rises with size within a family,** and is lowest for
  TriLM 99M and GPT-2.
- **Inverse hits are fewer than forward hits** for every model.
- **Identity (ident) hits exceed forward hits** for the instruction-tuned
  models and may not for the base models and the TriLMs.
- **raw_true is near 1 everywhere;** story_in is high and story_out shows
  collapse in the smaller models.
- **Invented countries draw a confident real capital from the smaller
  models** more often than from the larger.
- **The affirmation forms work only for the instruction-tuned models.** For
  base models these two kinds measure nothing, and their control will say
  so.

## Rules

As batches 0002 and 0003: existing tools; observations are steps with ids
and `continue-on-error`, with outcomes recorded from the workflow's own
`steps` context; GNU `time` on tool runs; nothing runs on the development
machine. Prompts, transformations and queries are data files in the
repository.

## What this does not establish

- Anything about relations other than *capital of*.
- Anything about multi-token answers beyond their first token.
- Calibration against anything but Wikidata's preference at the time of
  the fetch.
