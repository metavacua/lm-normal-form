# Batch 0004: integrity of one stored relation on a finite domain

- **Registered:** 2026-10-06, before any cell ran.
- **Status:** run; results below.
- **Branch:** `batch/0004-integrity`, on top of batch 0003.
  **Workflow:** `.github/workflows/batch-0004.yml`. **Data:** `batches/0004/`.
  **Queries:** `queries/0004/`.

## Purpose

If a language model is a database, its quality is integrity, not a benchmark
score. This batch measures the integrity of one relation, *capital of*, on
the finite domain of sovereign states, against a named authority at a
recorded time, for twelve models. It separates what is checked by logic
alone from what is checked against the authority, and it separates the
model's capability for fiction from its tendency to it.

## The pair (L, T)

- **T, the authority:** Wikidata, the capital (P36) of every sovereign state
  not dissolved, fetched at the start of the run; the time of the fetch is
  recorded. The **canonical** capital of a country is the one Wikidata
  itself prefers (its "truthy" statement). A country is **determined** when
  it has exactly one canonical capital with an English label; only
  determined countries are prompted. Every capital a country ever had is
  kept as well, with rank and validity, so that an answer can be canonical,
  historical, another country's capital, or no capital at all.
- **L, the logic:** the reflexivity of identity, read as a schema over
  terms, and persistence of a truth under extension of its context. Neither
  needs the authority.
- **Values:** every readout is a next-token distribution, the model's
  softmax over its vocabulary, so a number here is the model's mass on a
  token and nothing else until calibration says otherwise.

## Readouts

Each model answers about 1,800 prompts, one next-token distribution each,
with the twenty most probable tokens kept. Nine kinds per determined
country X with canonical capital C, paired with the next country Y in
alphabetical order, whose capital is CY:

- **fwd:** "The capital of X is" → ` C`.
- **inv:** "C is the capital of" → ` X`.
- **ident:** "The capital of X is the capital of" → ` X`.
- **persist:** "The capital of Y is CY. The capital of X is" → ` C`.
- **raw_true:** "The capital of X is C. The capital of X is" → ` C`.
- **story_in:** "In the story, the capital of X is CY. In the story, the
  capital of X is" → ` CY`.
- **story_out:** "In the story, the capital of X is CY. Outside the story,
  the capital of X is" → ` C`; ` CY` here is collapse.
- **affirm:** "True or false: the capital of X is the capital of X. Answer:"
  → ` True`.
- **affirm_ctrl:** "True or false: the capital of X is the capital of Y.
  Answer:" → ` False`.

And for twelve invented country names: **invented_fwd** (no target; a gap
is the right answer) and **invented_ident** (the name itself).

A first-ranked token counts as the target when it begins the target and is
longer than one character. Matching is by string prefix, so it does not
depend on any tokenizer.

## Models

- SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, SmolLM2-360M-Instruct, SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct, and
  SmolLM2-135M without instruction tuning.
- Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B.
- TriLM 99M, 390M and 1.1B: ternary weights, Apache-2.0.
- GPT-2 and GPT-2 medium.

Ten are read through llama.cpp, after conversion by its own converter
(float32, or float16 for Qwen3-4B and the TriLMs, whose ternary values
float16 holds exactly). GPT-2's checkpoints are refused by that converter
(batch 0002), so the two GPT-2 cells are read through PyTorch by one
invocation that loops over the prompt file. Batch 0003 showed the two
readouts agree to four decimal places.

## What the judge computes

In Oxigraph, over the authority graph, the prompt graph and every model's
answers:

- **hits:** per model and kind, how often the first-ranked token is the
  target, and the mean mass on the target.
- **reflexivity:** per model, the countries for which the forward and the
  inverse readouts both return (both ways), one does (forward only, inverse
  only), or neither.
- **soundness:** per model, forward answers that are canonical, historical,
  another country's capital (a glut), or no capital (a gap).
- **persistence:** per model, how many countries lose more than 0.1 of mass
  on the canonical capital when an irrelevant true sentence precedes the
  prompt.
- **identity:** per model, after "The capital of X is the capital of", the
  mass on X against the mass on the paired Y, since the first token there
  is mostly "the" or "which" and decides nothing. Added after the first
  judge run, when the ident hits turned out to say little.
- **story:** per model, inside the story: the story's capital, the
  canonical one asserted against the premise (override), or other; outside
  it: the canonical capital, the story's capital leaking out (collapse), or
  other. The first form reported only amnesia and collapse; the amnesia
  figure could not tell override from anything else.
- **invented:** per model, the mean confidence of the first-ranked token for
  invented countries and how often it is a real capital.
- **calibration:** per model, the first-ranked token's probability in bins
  of 0.2 against how often that token is the canonical capital. The first
  form binned the mass on the canonical capital against whether it was
  first-ranked, which asks nearly the same thing twice; replaced before
  the results were read as a whole.
- **top1:** every first-ranked answer, for the record.

## Expected

- **Forward soundness rises with size within a family,** and is lowest for
  TriLM 99M and GPT-2.
- **Inverse hits are fewer than forward hits** for every model.
- **Identity (ident) hits exceed forward hits** for the instruction-tuned
  models and may not for the base models and the TriLMs.
- **raw_true is near 1 everywhere;** story_in is high and story_out shows
  collapse in the smaller models.
- **Invented countries draw a confident real capital from the smaller
  models** more often than from the larger.
- **The affirmation forms work only for the instruction-tuned models.** For
  base models these two kinds measure nothing, and their control will say
  so.

## Rules

As batches 0002 and 0003: existing tools; observations are steps with ids
and `continue-on-error`, with outcomes recorded from the workflow's own
`steps` context; GNU `time` on tool runs; nothing runs on the development
machine. Prompts, transformations and queries are data files in the
repository.

## What this does not establish

- Anything about relations other than *capital of*.
- Anything about multi-token answers beyond their first token.
- Calibration against anything but Wikidata's preference at the time of
  the fetch.

The judge is its own workflow, `batch-0004-judge.yml`, called at the end
of a run and runnable alone against a recorded run, so that a question can
be asked again without the models being read again. It derives countries,
prompts and RDF from the run's recorded Wikidata answer with the
repository's current `jq` files and stops unless the derived prompts equal,
id for id and text for text, the prompts the models read.

### Reading

- **The reflexivity schema holds in every model, including the ones with
  no stored relation.** After "The capital of X is the capital of", every
  model prefers X to the paired Y in 161 to 174 of 189 countries, with Y
  preferred in 1 to 3; TriLM 99M and GPT-2, which name the canonical
  capital for 0 and 4 countries, prefer X in 161 and 174. Test L therefore
  measures something Test D does not: the identity form is carried by the
  language, independently of the content of the relation. That is what an
  axiom schema should do, and it is the first content-free integrity
  property this project has measured.
- **Soundness orders the models and separates kinds of failure.** Canonical
  first tokens: Qwen3-4B 179, SmolLM2-1.7B 177, SmolLM2-360M 172,
  Qwen3-1.7B 145, TriLM 1.1B 130, GPT-2 medium 89, Qwen3-0.6B 79,
  SmolLM2-135M-Instruct 62, TriLM 390M 48, SmolLM2-135M 22, GPT-2 4,
  TriLM 99M 0. Within each family it rises with size, as expected.
  Historical capitals are rare (0 to 4) and another country's capital
  rarer still (0 to 7): failures are gaps, not gluts. The small models do
  not store the wrong capital, they store none and continue with "the",
  "located" or "a".
- **The inverse relation is weaker than the forward one** for every model,
  as expected: both ways 127 of 189 for SmolLM2-1.7B against 177 forward,
  117 against 179 for Qwen3-4B. TriLM 1.1B is the exception in kind: 28
  countries inverse-only against 38 forward-only, the most symmetric
  store.
- **Fiction scoping fails in opposite directions by model class.** Base
  and small models enter the story (GPT-2 189, TriLM 1.1B 186, SmolLM2-135M
  187) and let it leak: collapse 169, 62, 35. The instruction-tuned and
  larger models keep the story out of the default graph (collapse 1 to 3)
  but assert the canonical capital inside the story in 53 to 94 countries:
  SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct 94, Qwen3-1.7B 67, Qwen3-4B 66. Qwen3-1.7B says
  "not" inside the story 16 times, contradicting the premise outright. In
  this sample the capability for fiction, holding a scoped premise, and
  the soundness of the store pull against each other; Qwen3-4B is the best
  compromise, 109 inside and 175 outside with 1 collapse. "Fictional" and
  "false" are kept apart here by construction: inside the story the
  story's capital is the proved answer and the canonical capital is the
  error.
- **Read-after-write in this form measures repetition, not retention.**
  "The capital of X is C. The capital of X is" gets "also" from Qwen3-4B
  164 times and from Qwen3-0.6B 161 times, a continuation consistent with
  the write, not a retraction. The models that "pass" are the copiers
  (GPT-2 187, TriLM 1.1B 187). The next form must write in one form and
  read in another.
- **The "distractor" is a demonstration.** Mass on the canonical capital
  rises for every model when another country's true sentence precedes the
  prompt (TriLM 1.1B 0.30 → 0.68, Qwen3-1.7B 0.41 → 0.84), because the
  sentence shows the form of the answer. Persistence proper needs an
  irrelevant sentence of a different form; the drop count (0 to 22) is
  the only part of this readout that bears on persistence.
- **The affirmation control works.** Qwen3-1.7B and 4B answer True to the
  tautology and False to its control 189 times each; SmolLM2-1.7B 85 and
  167; SmolLM2-135M base says False to both, 0 and 189, and the control
  exposes it; the rest answer neither, as expected for base models and
  the TriLMs.
- **Invented countries draw a gap, not a glut.** First tokens are "the" or
  "a" with mean probability 0.10 to 0.40; a real capital is given 0 to 2
  times in 12. The hypothesis that small models offer confident real
  capitals for invented names is not supported at the first token.
- **The identity prompt itself is a source of fictional expressions.**
  SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct answers "The capital of France is the capital of"
  with " Europe" at 0.412 before " the" 0.317 and " France" 0.093: a
  coherent, unanchored expression the authority cannot evaluate, which is
  the class the batch set out to find.

### Faults of the design, for the next batch

- The hit criterion is a first-token prefix; " B" counts for Bamako and
  for Berlin alike. Multi-token decoding or teacher-forced likelihood of
  the whole name would remove the ambiguity.
- raw_true and persist need the forms described above.
- The judge stores probabilities as `xsd:double` in the default value
  algebra; the declared algebra per readout and the datatype per tensor
  (`docs/research/questions.md`) are not yet in the graph.
