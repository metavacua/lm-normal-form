## Purpose

R3 of the research questions (`docs/research/questions.md` on the `docs/foundations` branch: what is the normal form of a language
model) lists "what the normalising rewrites are" as open. For the weights, a normal form is a canonical form under the symmetries of
the architecture. The symmetries of a Llama-style decoder that are signed permutations and powers of two (the feed-forward units, the
heads, the hidden coordinates, and signs and powers of two on the dimensions of the value/output and query/key pairs) are operations on
the stored numbers that move entries, negate them or scale them by 2^k, and they are symmetries of the function in real arithmetic;
in the arithmetic of a run, the signs and powers of two give the same bits and the permutations change the order of sums (P11).
Finding a canonical form under them is a canonical labelling problem: a matrix under A -> PAQ is a bipartite weighted graph, and
canonical labelling of a bipartite graph is as hard as graph isomorphism in general. Graph isomorphism is not known to be in P; Babai gave
a quasipolynomial-time algorithm, and canonical-labelling programs (nauty, bliss, Traces) are standard and fast on most inputs. None of
that is needed if the case at hand is special.

The order of work is the one asked for: the first check is to try to show that the weights are **not** the special case, that is, to
look for two unlike items that no signature tells apart; if there are none, the special case is solved by sorting and the solution is
tested against the group and against non-symmetries; if there are, the problem escalates, from the one matrix to the network's shared
index spaces, then to refinement, then to search, and this batch goes as far as refinement and says where it stops.

**A statement of mine earlier in this thread was wrong, and is corrected here.** My table of normal forms said that for permutation
equivalence "sorting rows then columns works when the rows are distinct", and a text pasted into the thread gives the same procedure. It does
not. Take A = [[1, 5], [2, 3], [2, 4]], whose rows and columns are all distinct, and A Q with Q exchanging the columns: sorting
the rows and then the columns gives [[1, 5], [2, 3], [2, 4]] for A and [[2, 3], [2, 4], [1, 5]] for A Q. On 1,500 random real
matrices of 12 x 20 (all rows and columns distinct), each in two random images P A Q, "sort the rows, then the columns" gave different
matrices for the two images in 1,430 of 1,500 pairs. What works is to order the rows by a signature that does not depend on the order of
the columns (the row's sorted entries) and then to sort the columns as vectors: 0 failures in the 1,500 pairs. That procedure needs the
signatures of the rows to differ, and on 1,500 random 12 x 20 matrices of the entries 0, 1, 2, where they often tie, it failed in 761
(and sorting rows then columns in 1,499). (The trials are the last checks of the self-test, cell 00, with a fixed seed; the counts are its
output on the development machine, before this registration, and are not predictions; the cells below repeat the question on real
weights.) The hardness of graph
isomorphism is the worst case; the question for weights is whether signatures tell the items apart.

No LARQL code, text or data is used in this batch.

## The ladder, and what is checked

The special case, for a group that acts on index spaces (sets of items: the rows and columns of a matrix, or the feed-forward units
of a layer, shared by three matrices), is that every item can be told from every other by a signature, so that sorting by signature
is a canonical form and nothing is searched. The levels, each stronger in what it uses and weaker in what it needs:

- **L0, one matrix, independent row and column permutations.** The items are the rows and the columns; the signature of a row is its
  sorted entries. Checked: how many rows (columns) there are, how many are distinct as vectors, how many distinct by signature. A
  signature tie between rows that differ as vectors ("unlike items") is where the textbook special case fails; identical items tie
  harmlessly, since exchanging them changes nothing.
- **L1, the network's own group.** The index spaces are shared: a feed-forward unit is a row of the gate matrix, a row of the up matrix
  and a column of the down matrix; a hidden coordinate is a column of every matrix that reads the residual stream and a row of every
  one that writes it. (a) *Without an anchor*: a unit's **joint signature**, the sorted triples (gate entry, product of the up entry and
  the down entry, magnitude of the up entry over the largest) over the hidden coordinates, which does not depend on the order of the
  coordinates or on the unit's sign or power of two. (b) *With the vocabulary as an anchor*: the tokens are labelled, not permutable, so the
  columns of the embedding, in token order, order the hidden coordinates (up to sign) if they are pairwise distinct; then every unit and
  every head is a vector in that order, and an item is told apart if its vector differs.
- **L2, refinement.** Where the joint signature ties, rounds of colour refinement on the graph of units and coordinates (edges labelled by
  the triple). **L3** is search (individualization and refinement) and is not implemented: a tie that survives L2 between unlike items
  ends the batch's construction and is reported.

## The hypothesis, and what would refute it

**H.** In each of five models (SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, SmolLM2-135M, FloatLM 99M, and the ternary TriLM 99M and TriLM 390M), every
permutable index space is in the special case at L1: the hidden coordinates are anchored; the units of every layer are told apart by their
joint signature in the first round, with no anchor; the heads and key/value groups are told apart; so that the canonical form of
`canon.py` (sorting by digests of the items, after fixing the coordinates by the embedding, with exact normalizations of signs and
powers of two) exists and is exact for the group of `symmetry.py`.

H is refuted by an index space of a model in which two unlike items have the same signature at the level H names, and by a random
element of the group that the canonical form does not undo, bit for bit.

## The claim it would license, and nothing more

If the predictions hold, the weights of these five models have a canonical form under the signed permutations and powers of two of the
architecture, computed by sorting, and the digest of that form (batch 0011's) is an identity of the weights up to those symmetries; the
two roots of one model's canonical tensors are the same as those of any image of it under the group. It licenses nothing about the continuous
symmetries (the GL(hd) of the value/output pair, rotations of the residual stream), about the gauge of the norm weights (not normalized
here), about models that have exactly duplicated items beyond those the check reports, or about other architectures.

## Cells

One job for the tiny random Llama first (the smoke: the self-test and every cell, no claim), then the five models in parallel. The
self-test (cell 00) runs a random toy Llama in float64, with a float64 RMSNorm (Hugging Face's casts to float32 inside, which hides any
difference below 1e-8): every action of the group is a symmetry of the function, the canonical form of three random images of the model
equals the model's bit for bit and is idempotent and preserves the function, a non-symmetry is seen, "sort rows then columns" fails on
the three-by-two example, the signature procedure fails on a matrix with a tie, and the 1,500 pairs of "Purpose" are run again.

- **01 L0.** For every class of matrix (q, k, v, o, gate, up, down, embedding): the rows and columns of each matrix, distinct as vectors
  and by signature.
- **02 L1.** The coordinates (the embedding's columns, distinct up to sign); every layer's units by the separate signatures (the
  sorted gate row, up-down products, up magnitudes), by the joint signature, and anchored; every layer's heads and groups anchored and
  index-free; the refinement where the joint signature ties.
- **03 The canonical form.** Random elements of the whole group (8; 3 for TriLM 390M) and of each part alone (2; 1 for TriLM 390M): the
  digest of every canonical tensor against that of the model's; and four non-symmetries: one gate row negated; one unit's gate and
  up rows exchanged; one float32 ulp in one query weight; two embedding columns exchanged and nothing else.
- **04 "Sort rows then columns".** Layer 0's q, k and gate matrices under 12 random (P, Q): the number of distinct outputs of the naive
  procedure and of the signature procedure; and a 12 x 20 matrix of 0, 1, 2 with two rows alike in signature.
- **05 The function** (SmolLM2-135M-Instruct only). The group's action, a part at a time and whole, applied to the weights (float32), and
  the model run in float32 on 63 sequences against the unchanged model: the largest difference of the logits, the Kullback-Leibler divergence,
  the share of positions with the same argmax. Hub revisions are pinned in the workflow (below).

## Auxiliaries

What a refutation could land on instead of H, and which would be blamed first:

- the signatures: sorted entries (L0); the triples and their invariance to the unit's sign and power of two (L1); a signature that is too
  weak reads as a tie, one that is too strong (a digest of the vector) hides it, which is why both are reported;
- the canonical form's own rules (the sign of the first non-zero entry in canonical coordinate order; the power of two that puts the largest
  entry in [1, 2)), which are conventions and must be invariant, and the digests (blake2b, 128 bits) by which items are ordered;
- the group action in `symmetry.py`, written by the same hand as the canonical form: a flaw common to both could make P7 hold by
  accident, which P11 (the action is a symmetry of the function) and P9 (the canonical form can fail) are for;
- the widening of float16 and bfloat16 to float32, which is exact, and the digest of a tensor of batch 0011;
- signed zeros, which matter for the ternary models: the canonical form is compared bit for bit, and a flip of sign turns +0.0 into -0.0,
  which the inverse flip turns back (the sign bit of a product is the exclusive or of the sign bits); the signatures read -0.0 as 0.0;
  the only test of this before the run is the tiny model (17 zeros), so cell 03 on TriLM is its test;
- for P11 the kernels of PyTorch's CPU backend: a bit-exact result needs a fixed order of sums for every shape;
- the evaluation set: 60 sentences of my own and batch 0010's three prompts;
- the memory of the TriLM 390M cell (the model in float32, an image of it, its canonical form: about 7 GB by my count, on a 16 GB runner):
  a cell that is killed is not graded, and is not a refutation.

## Rivals to exclude

- **The special case is an artifact of the signature chosen.** The anchored check uses the whole vector, the strongest signature; the index-free
  one is weaker and is what H asks of the units. Both are reported.
- **The canonical form is canonical only because the two halves share a mistake.** See the auxiliaries; the function check and the
  non-symmetries are the controls.
- **Ties that are identical items.** Exchanging identical items changes nothing; they are counted separately from unlike items.

## Grading, and what is not done

A prediction is graded from the JSON that the cells keep, in the artifacts of the run named in the results. A cell that does not run
leaves its predictions ungraded, and that is stated; it does not refute them. A fault found in the code after this commit is declared
before the run that follows it, with the change, and the predictions are not edited. Not done: search (L3); the continuous symmetries;
the gauge of the norm weights; the canonical form of a model with a bias or with a different attention pattern; any model larger than
390M.
