## Purpose

A pasted LLM-written overview ("When you rigorously formulate a Transformer as an algebraic operator ...") makes four groups of claims about Transformers as algebraic objects: permutation
equivariance and "weight encryption" by a secret permutation; SVD, LoRA and spectral analysis of attention circuits; the Cayley-Hamilton theorem as a path to layer pruning; group theory,
invariant subspaces and basis change. The request was to formalize it programmatically, decompose it into testable and provable claims that yield executable programs with language-model runtime
behaviour as their demonstration, and to develop what survives. This batch takes the first group and the symmetry content of the fourth: every statement about which transformations of the weights leave
the function unchanged, and what follows for obfuscating a model. The second group is batch 0016's, the third batch 0017's; the survivors of all of them are developed in batch 0018.

Six literature searches were made first by research agents (their notes are in the session's research folder; the author did not read the cited sources, so the citations below are the agents' reports). What they changed in the plan:

- *The statements are mostly published; the exact certification is not found.* The symmetry facts (no privileged residual basis, the orthogonal gauge with folded norms, the GL(d_head) gauge of attention
  and its restriction to complex scalars under RoPE, conditional linearity given frozen gates) are in Elhage et al. 2021, SliceGPT, QuaRot, Tran et al. (ICLR 2025), Wang and Wang, Golden (2025). Random testing
  over finite fields to decide equivalence of tensor programs is standard (PET, TASO, Mirage at OSDI 2025, Gulwani and Necula's random interpretation). I found no earlier use of an exact F_p forward pass of
  a Llama-style model to certify weight-space symmetries; an absence in searches is not a proof of absence.
- *"For every nonlinearity" needs random oracles, not a polynomial.* My first version of the formal tier used polynomial surrogates for exp, SiLU and the norm function and said that a pass proved the structure for every
  nonlinearity. It proves it for those polynomials. The version below has both, and says which claim each proves.
- *The pasted attribution of the Cayley-Hamilton claim* is reported by the agents to be wrong (arXiv 2407.00958 is said to be a universal-approximation paper whose pruning remark is a triangle inequality); not checked by the author; batch 0017 takes that up.

## The formal tier

**One definition, three arithmetics.** `model.py` is a Llama-style decoder (pre-norm; grouped-query attention with optional rotary embeddings in the rotate-half pairing and an optional causal mask; a gated block;
a final norm; a head that is the embedding or its own matrix) written once, generic over the arithmetic of `ops.py`:

- `FloatOps`: float64 with the real exp, SiLU, 1/sqrt(mean square + eps) and cos and sin. Run on Hugging Face's weights it is checked against Transformers (`conform.py`).
- `FieldOps`: F_p, p = 2^61 - 1, with polynomial surrogates. A pass proves the identity for those polynomials.
- `OracleFieldOps`: F_p, p = 2^127 - 1, with exp, SiLU and the norm function each a lazily sampled random function (a memoized table), shared by both sides of a comparison.

**What a pass proves.** Let two programs P1 and P2 (the model and the model transformed by g) be run on random parameters, a random transformation and the same oracles. If they agree, then with the probability given below the two
agree as rational functions of the parameters and the transformation's entries *for every function in the place of each oracle*: the arguments of every oracle call on one side are those of the matching call on the other, and
everything downstream is the same ring computation. (The argument: treat each oracle output as a fresh indeterminate; two calls with different argument functions that collide in value at the random point are the bad event, at most
(D_i + D_j)/p each by Schwartz-Zippel; with no collision the final difference is a polynomial in the parameters and the oracle outputs, nonzero with probability at most N/p of vanishing. It is the finite-field form of
random interpretation with uninterpreted functions. The argument is mine and has not been checked in a proof assistant.) An identity that holds as a formal identity holds in the reals for every nonlinearity, with integer
constants and a field larger than twice the largest coefficient. `degree.py` computes a bound for each claim by running the same claim code over an arithmetic of degrees: for the 46 claims, at most **2.6e-31** per trial
for the oracle arithmetic, and at most 6.6e-8 for the polynomial one (whose degree compounds from layer to layer, 135 times per layer in an independent measurement). These are heuristic bounds: they rest on the unchecked argument above and on the bookkeeping of `degree.py`, and no prediction is graded against them.

**What it does not prove.** Anything that needs a particular property of exp, SiLU or 1/sqrt (positivity, homogeneity, exp(a+b) = exp(a)exp(b)); anything about float32 or bfloat16 (the float64 and real-model runs are for that); anything that depends on
the order of the sums; order-dependent operations (max-subtraction in a softmax, a mask as minus infinity; here the mask is a structural zero); the correspondence between model.py and a real model, which is by inspection and by `conform.py`.

**The generators and the counterexamples** (`gauge.py`): a signed permutation of the hidden coordinates (norm weights permuted, not negated); a per-group invertible matrix on the value dimensions with the inverse on the o columns of the group's
heads; a per-group per-plane complex scalar on keys with its inverse transpose on queries (RoPE: the commutant of the rotations; the rotation by position is a power of an element of SO(2, F_p), p = 3 mod 4, so the commutant is a field);
a permutation and nonzero scales of the gated block's units on the up rows and the down columns; head and group permutations; norm scales with the inverse on the columns of the readers; the orthogonal group after folding the norm weights
into the readers (Householder products over F_p, orthogonal for v^T v != 0); the same in the unfolded parameterization with the readers conjugated; and the wrong versions: a body-only permutation, the pasted rule, an unfolded rotation, a
scale on the gate rows, a value-only gauge, a general GL(d_head) under RoPE, a head permutation across groups, a rotation with a tied head and a learned final norm, and the centering of the embedding rows.

**The 46 claims** are in `claims.py` and in `docs/batches/0015/claims.tsv` (id, family, expected outcome, scope, statement, source): the token permutations (T), the hidden coordinates (H), attention (A), the feed-forward block and the norms (M, N), the composition (C), and the KV cache (K: unchanged, permuted, mapped by the value matrix, mapped by the key-plane scalars, with the counterexamples); each is run in the three arithmetics and must come out as expected in all: those that expect equality equal
in every trial, those that expect a difference different in every trial.

**The dimension of the symmetry group.** `symdim.py` computes, by the complex step, the Jacobian of the logits (or log-probabilities) of many random sequences with respect to all parameters of a small model and counts the rank deficiency:
the dimension of the fibre of the function through a generic point, which contains the orbit of every continuous symmetry. The number that is to be compared with the deficiency, at generic parameters, is

  L (n_kv hd^2 + q + F) + norms d + [untied] d(d-1)/2 + [eps = 0] + [untied, log-probabilities] d,

where the terms `L (...)`, `norms d` and the rotation term count the generators of `gauge.py` (the value/output gauge, the complex scalars on the rotary planes, the unit scales, the norm scalings, the orthogonal rotation), the term `[eps = 0]` is a global positive scale of the stream and the last term is a shift of the logits by a vector, for neither of which `gauge.py` has a generator, and with q = n_kv hd with RoPE and n_kv hd^2 without, norms = (2L + 1) for an untied head and 2L for a tied one (a tied head forbids the gauge of the final norm and the rotation, since the tie forces E Q on the head and the rotation would need
E diag(g) Q diag(g)^-1). For SmolLM2-135M (L = 30, n_kv = 3, hd = 64, F = 1,536, d = 576, tied) with eps > 0 this is 30 x (12,288 + 192 + 1,536) + 60 x 576 = **455,040**, which a count in the research notes (an agent's) also gave.
The seven configurations of `symdim.CONFIGS` (d = 6, vocabulary 7 = d + 1, which an earlier numerical rank study of mine, on 21 small decoders and with the same formula, found necessary for the count to be exact) predict, in order: 142, 148, 141, 121, 120, 82, 190. The rank threshold is `1e-8` times the largest singular value, at one random point per configuration (seed 15).

**What the results say, and what they do not.**

- *The group.* For every symmetric gauge of the list, on a trained 30-layer model, the function is unchanged to 3e-12 in float64 and 1e-4 in float32 (all generations identical), and the formula for the dimension of the continuous symmetry group is exact in the seven small configurations tested, with and without tied head, positions, grouped-query attention, a positive epsilon in the norm and the log-probability output. The structural terms are the generators of `gauge.py`; the terms for epsilon zero (a positive scale of the stream) and for the shift of the log-probabilities have no generator in `gauge.py` and were taken from the earlier rank study. This is numerical evidence that the list of generators is complete at the level of the Lie algebra for these configurations (a Jacobian rank at one generic point per configuration, d = 6, float64, models with vocabulary d + 1; the rank threshold 1e-8 times the largest singular value is arbitrary but lies inside an empty gap of 5e8 to 9e11 between the counted and the uncounted singular values); it is not a theorem, not a statement about the discrete part, and not a measurement on SmolLM2 itself (the formula gives 455,040 there).
- *The cache.* The KV cache follows the claims on real weights to 1e-13: unchanged by every symmetry of the stream, the units and the norms (to 7e-15), permuted, mapped by the value matrix, mapped by the key-plane scalars. The gauges that move it move it by 0.8 to 196 times the largest entry of the original's cache (the value/output gauge, with its condition number). So a hidden-dimension permutation, even with a secret key, leaves the cache of the keyed model equal to the original's (2e-15): nothing of the key is in it.
- *The "weight encryption" claim.* As worded it is false (the body permuted alone gives perplexity 7.5e11; the consistent permutation with the key is exactly the original), and as security it fails when the public base of the model is known: matching the permuted body (here a fine-tune) against its public base model recovered the whole key (576 of 576) in 13 seconds and restored the perplexity to every digit; a checkpoint with no public parent was not tried. A linear adapter at the input and output of the body, trained for 300 steps without the base and without the key, brought the training-window perplexity from 4.8e10 (the broken model) to about 50 to 60 (48.6 at its minimum within 300 steps, 195 s on 4 CPUs); **this is in-sample (6 windows of 128 tokens against 664,000 adapter parameters, evaluated on the windows it was trained on, the trace is not monotone) and says nothing about held-out text**; a held-out evaluation was not run.
- *Precision.* In bfloat16, even a pure signed permutation (the same numbers in another order) changes the logits by 0.63 and leaves 1 of 4 generations unchanged: the rounding noise of a bfloat16 forward pass of this model is of order 1 logit, so bfloat16 cannot certify exactness, and the cost of an ill-conditioned gauge is visible in it only at kappa 1,000 (8.3 against a floor of 1.0). Q11's bfloat16 thresholds were set from an error model (error proportional to kappa) that overstated the effect: the attention output is a small part of the stream and the floor is high. In float32 the effect is real and grows with kappa, by 1.35, 1.7 and 4.8 times per decade (5.0e-5 at kappa 1, 5.5e-4 at kappa 1,000: 11 times), which is less than proportional.
- *A published practice.* Subtracting the mean from the embedding rows, which the `fuse_layer_norms` of QuaRot and SpinQuant do for every model type according to the research agents' reading of the code (not read by the author), changes the logits of SmolLM2-135M-Instruct by 0.86 and breaks 3 of 4 generations: not function-preserving for an RMSNorm model (the claim H8 proves it over F_p, H8n shows it exact for LayerNorm). What those codebases do to Llama models in practice was not examined here.
- *Optimizers.* The symmetries of the function are not all symmetries of the training dynamics; this follows from the update rules (an orthogonal matrix commutes with SGD, only signed permutations with Adam, a scaling of the units with neither), and a 100-step float64 run on a tiny model illustrates it (one seed, one learning rate each; the scaling row mixes signs and scales 2^k).
- *Not done:* a held-out evaluation of the adapter attack; the symmetry dimension of a trained model; other models than SmolLM2-135M-Instruct for the real-checkpoint cells (the base model was used only as the public reference of the attack); LayerNorm models; the claims of the pasted overview about SVD, LoRA and Cayley-Hamilton (batches 0016 and 0017).
